# Technical English — Course 3, Semester 1 — Lessons 6–8
## Aviation industry, aircraft structure and materials

**Status:** new lesson drafts for the provisional five-year course map; not yet imported into the portal.  
**Level:** B1–B1+ ESP bridge. Students should already have studied the Basic English sequence and the existing Technical English lessons on materials, metal processes, technical drawing and CAD/CAM.  
**Professional groups:** technologists, programmers, electricians and machine-tool operators.  
**Audio:** original Spotlight-style listening scripts are included as text for recording; no MP3 files or player are included.

## Learning outcomes for the block

Students will be able to:
- describe the main sectors of the aviation industry and broad aircraft categories;
- identify major aircraft structural parts in an introductory text;
- describe the function of the fuselage, wings and control surfaces at a basic language-learning level;
- classify materials by broad families and compare properties;
- read a simplified materials note and identify information relevant to manufacturing, inspection, electrical installation and digital systems;
- report a technical issue clearly and avoid treating classroom examples as real maintenance instructions.

# Lesson 6. The Aviation Industry: Aircraft Types and Main Sectors

**Suggested time:** 90 minutes

## Learning outcomes

Students will:
1. identify major sectors of the aviation industry;
2. distinguish civil, military, cargo, passenger and special-purpose aircraft at an introductory level;
3. understand common aviation industry vocabulary;
4. extract specific information from a short industry overview;
5. listen to a discussion about different jobs in an aircraft production chain;
6. describe how four college specialisms contribute to an aviation project.

## 1. Lead-in: what is the aviation industry?

Discuss:
- What organisations are involved before an aircraft enters service?
- What is the difference between aircraft design, manufacturing, operation and maintenance?
- Which college specialisms could contribute to aircraft production?
- Why do different teams need shared technical documentation?

Build a simple chain:

**Design → materials and components → manufacturing → assembly and integration → inspection and testing → operation and support**

This is a simplified educational overview, not a complete regulatory process.

## 2. Vocabulary

| English | Russian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| aviation industry | авиационная промышленность / отрасль | The aviation industry includes many different activities. |
| aircraft | воздушное судно | An aircraft may be designed for passengers or cargo. |
| airframe | планер воздушного судна | The airframe is the main structural body. |
| manufacturer | производитель | The manufacturer builds the aircraft. |
| supplier | поставщик | A supplier provides components or materials. |
| assembly | сборка | Final assembly brings major sections together. |
| component | компонент, деталь | Each component has a defined function. |
| passenger aircraft | пассажирский самолёт | Passenger aircraft carry people. |
| cargo aircraft | грузовой самолёт | Cargo aircraft transport goods. |
| helicopter | вертолёт | A helicopter uses rotating blades for lift. |
| general aviation | авиация общего назначения | General aviation includes many non-airline activities. |
| unmanned aircraft | беспилотное воздушное судно | An unmanned aircraft operates without a pilot on board. |
| certification | сертификация | Certification confirms compliance with applicable requirements. |
| supply chain | цепочка поставок | The supply chain includes manufacturers and suppliers. |
| quality assurance | обеспечение качества | Quality assurance supports consistent production. |

### Aircraft categories

| Category | Typical purpose |
|---|---|
| passenger aircraft | carrying passengers |
| cargo aircraft | transporting freight |
| business aircraft | private or corporate transport |
| training aircraft | pilot training |
| helicopter | transport, rescue, observation and other tasks |
| unmanned aircraft | remote or autonomous missions, depending on design |
| military aircraft | defence-related roles |

Aircraft categories can overlap; classify them by purpose and design context rather than assuming every aircraft fits only one label.

2. assembly  
3. cargo aircraft  
4. certification  
5. quality assurance

a) a process that provides confidence that requirements are met

## 3. Reading: one aircraft, many teams

Read:

“An aircraft is the result of work carried out by many organisations and professional teams. Designers develop the overall concept and define requirements. Materials specialists select suitable materials for different parts. Manufacturing teams produce components using approved processes. Technologists prepare process documentation and help ensure that operations are repeatable. Programmers may develop or support digital tools used for design, production planning, data processing or equipment control. Electricians work with electrical systems, wiring documentation and test equipment within their authorised scope. Machine-tool operators produce parts to specified dimensions and record relevant production information.

The teams depend on accurate drawings, specifications and revisions. A change to one component may affect other parts of the design or production process. For this reason, information must be controlled and communicated clearly.”

### First reading — gist

Choose the best summary:
A) Aircraft production depends on coordinated work between different teams and controlled technical information.  
B) One specialist completes every task independently.  
C) Aircraft production does not require drawings or records.

### Second reading — details

1. What do materials specialists do?
2. What is one task of technologists?
3. Give two examples of digital work mentioned in the text.
4. What do electricians work with?
5. What is the machine-tool operator expected to produce?
6. Why must drawings and revisions be controlled?

### Practice 2. Identify the professional group

Write **T** (technologist), **P** (programmer), **E** (electrician) or **M** (machine-tool operator).

1. Develops a digital tool for processing production data.
2. Produces a component to specified dimensions.
3. Prepares a process sheet for a manufacturing operation.
4. Works with wiring documentation and electrical test equipment.

Some tasks can involve more than one profession; choose the best match for the description.

## 4. Language focus: describing purpose

Use:
- **is used for + noun / -ing:** A cargo aircraft is used for transporting freight.
- **is designed to + verb:** A training aircraft is designed to support pilot training.
- **is responsible for + noun / -ing:** The supplier is responsible for providing the specified component.
- **provides / supports / carries / produces:** The team provides documentation.

### Practice 3. Complete the sentences

1. A cargo aircraft is designed to ______ freight.
2. A process sheet is used for ______ the manufacturing sequence.
3. A supplier is responsible for ______ components that meet the specification.
4. Quality assurance supports ______ production.

## 5. Listening: a college team discusses aviation careers

**Before you listen**

Key words: *production planning, component, process sheet, data, wiring, dimensions, inspection*. Predict what each specialist might contribute to a shared aircraft project.

**First listening — gist**

What are the students discussing?

A) How different professional roles contribute to aircraft production.  
B) How to book a holiday.  
C) How to repair a household appliance.

**Second listening — details**

1. What does the technologist prepare?
   A) a process sheet  
   B) a passenger ticket  
   C) a weather forecast
2. What does the programmer help develop?
   A) a digital tool for production data  
   B) a new uniform  
   C) a paper map
3. What does the electrician read?
   A) wiring documentation  
   B) a restaurant menu  
   C) a travel brochure
4. What does the machine-tool operator check?
   A) dimensions against the drawing  
   B) passenger names  
   C) the flight timetable

**Recording script — reveal after listening**

**Student 1:** Our class is preparing a presentation about jobs in aircraft production. Which roles should we include?

**Student 2:** A technologist can prepare the process sheet and explain the sequence of manufacturing operations.

**Student 3:** A programmer may support the digital side. For example, programmers can develop tools for production planning or process data.

**Student 4:** Electricians work with electrical documentation, wiring information and appropriate test equipment.

**Student 1:** And machine-tool operators produce parts to the dimensions shown on the drawing.

**Student 2:** Exactly. The teams need the same current information. If one drawing is changed, everyone affected must know which revision to use.

**Student 3:** So our presentation should show both the separate responsibilities and the communication between teams.

**Student 4:** Yes. No single role completes the whole aircraft.

**Language focus**
- *can prepare / may support* — describing possible professional duties;
- *to the dimensions shown on the drawing* — technical reference;
- *which revision to use* — indirect question;
- *everyone affected* — concise reference to relevant people.

**After listening**

In groups, prepare a four-part poster: technologist, programmer, electrician, machine-tool operator. For each role, give two tasks and one document or information source used by that specialist.

## 6. Speaking: explain the production chain

Use this structure:
1. First, the design team…
2. Next, materials are…
3. Then, components are…
4. During assembly…
5. Finally, the product is…

Add one sentence about why communication between teams matters.

## 7. Self-check and teacher key

4. Practice 1: 1 b; 2 c; 3 d; 4 e; 5 a.

# Lesson 7. Aircraft Structure: Fuselage, Wings and Control Surfaces

**Suggested time:** 90 minutes

## Learning outcomes

Students will:
1. identify major external structural parts of a conventional fixed-wing aircraft;
2. understand basic vocabulary for the fuselage, wings, tail and control surfaces;
3. describe location and function using simple present forms and prepositions;
4. read a simplified aircraft structure description;
5. listen to a learner-friendly explanation and identify component functions;
6. explain a diagram using accurate, non-operational language.

## 1. Lead-in

Look at an aircraft diagram supplied by the teacher. Identify any parts you already know. Discuss:
- Which part carries passengers or cargo?
- Which parts contribute to lift and directional control?
- Why do technical teams use standard names for components?

**Important scope note:** This is an introductory English lesson. It does not teach aircraft maintenance, inspection acceptance criteria or flight-control adjustment.

## 2. Vocabulary

| English | Russian | Basic description |
|---|---|---|
| fuselage | фюзеляж | the main body of an aircraft |
| wing | крыло | a structure that produces lift |
| tail | хвостовая часть | the rear part of an aircraft |
| horizontal stabilizer | горизонтальный стабилизатор | part of the tail assembly |
| vertical stabilizer | вертикальный стабилизатор | vertical surface on the tail |
| aileron | элерон | a movable control surface on a wing |
| elevator | руль высоты | a movable control surface associated with pitch control |
| rudder | руль направления | a movable control surface associated with yaw control |
| flap | закрылок | a high-lift device on a wing |
| landing gear | шасси | supports the aircraft on the ground |
| engine | двигатель | provides propulsion |
| cockpit | кабина экипажа | area where pilots operate the aircraft |
| cabin | пассажирская кабина | area for passengers in many aircraft |
| leading edge | передняя кромка | front edge of a wing |
| trailing edge | задняя кромка | rear edge of a wing |

### Location phrases

- at the front / at the rear;
- on the left / on the right;
- above / below;
- along the wing;
- attached to;
- located behind / in front of.

## 3. Reading: a simplified description

Read:

“The fuselage is the main body of a conventional fixed-wing aircraft. It contains the cockpit and, depending on the aircraft, a passenger cabin or cargo area. The wings extend from the fuselage and contribute to lift. The tail assembly includes stabilising surfaces and movable control surfaces. On many conventional aircraft, the ailerons are located near the outer parts of the wings, while the elevator and rudder are part of the tail control arrangement. The landing gear supports the aircraft when it is on the ground. Engines provide propulsion; their position and number depend on the aircraft design.”

### First reading — gist

A) The text identifies major aircraft structures and gives their general functions.  
B) The text gives a step-by-step maintenance procedure.  
C) The text describes airport catering.

### Second reading — details

1. What is the fuselage?
2. What may be located inside it?
3. What is the general function of the wings?
4. Where are ailerons commonly located?
5. Which control surfaces are associated with the tail?
6. What does the landing gear do?
7. Why can engine position vary?

### Practice 1. Match the component and function

1. fuselage  
2. wing  
3. landing gear  
4. engine  
5. rudder

a) provides propulsion  
b) main body of the aircraft  
c) supports the aircraft on the ground  
d) contributes to lift  
e) a control surface associated with yaw

## 4. Language focus: relative position and function

Useful structures:
- **X is located at/in/on…**
- **X is attached to…**
- **X is used to…**
- **X helps to…**
- **X is part of…**

Examples:
- The cockpit is located in the forward part of the fuselage.
- The wings are attached to the fuselage.
- The landing gear supports the aircraft on the ground.
- The rudder is part of the tail control arrangement.

### Practice 2. Complete the description

Use: *fuselage, wings, landing gear, engine, tail*.

1. The ______ is the main body of the aircraft.
2. The ______ contribute to lift.
3. The ______ supports the aircraft on the ground.
4. The ______ provides propulsion.
5. The ______ assembly is located at the rear.

## 5. Listening: describing an aircraft diagram

**Before you listen**

Review: *forward, rear, above, below, attached to, control surface*. Predict which parts the speaker will identify first.

**First listening — gist**

What is the speaker doing?

A) Describing the main parts of an aircraft diagram.  
B) Giving airport security instructions.  
C) Explaining how to buy a ticket.

**Second listening — details**

1. What is the main body called?
   A) fuselage  B) aileron  C) landing gear
2. What extends from the fuselage?
   A) wings  B) cabin seats  C) the rudder only
3. Which part is associated with yaw control?
   A) rudder  B) flap  C) cockpit
4. What supports the aircraft on the ground?
   A) landing gear  B) elevator  C) wingtip

**Recording script**

**Instructor:** Look at the simplified aircraft diagram. The main body is called the fuselage. The cockpit is in the forward part.

**Student:** The wings are attached to the fuselage, one on each side?

**Instructor:** Correct. The wings contribute to lift. Near the rear of the aircraft, you can see the tail assembly.

**Student:** Is the rudder part of the tail?

**Instructor:** Yes. The rudder is associated with yaw control. The elevator is also part of the tail control arrangement and is associated with pitch control.

**Student:** And the landing gear?

**Instructor:** It supports the aircraft when it is on the ground. Remember that aircraft designs differ, so always read the diagram title and its labels.

**Student:** So this is a general diagram, not a technical instruction.

**Instructor:** Exactly. It helps us learn the vocabulary and identify the main structures.

**Language focus**
- *is called* — naming;
- *is attached to* — relationship;
- *is associated with* — careful functional description;
- *designs differ* — qualification.

**After listening**

Students take turns describing a labelled diagram. The listener marks whether the speaker identifies the fuselage, wings, tail, one control surface and landing gear correctly.

## 6. Integrated task: four professional viewpoints

Use the same simplified aircraft structure diagram.

- **Technologist:** name a structural part and describe why its material and manufacturing process matter.
- **Programmer:** explain how a digital drawing or product-data system can store component information.
- **Electrician:** identify why electrical routing information must be coordinated with the structure (do not invent wiring routes).
- **Machine-tool operator:** explain why a drawing must specify dimensions and tolerances for a manufactured component.

Each student gives 3–4 sentences. Do not describe real aircraft repair or modification.

## 7. Self-check and teacher key

1. What is the fuselage?
2. Which structure contributes to lift?
3. What does landing gear do?
4. Practice 1: 1 b; 2 d; 3 c; 4 a; 5 e.
5. Practice 2: 1 fuselage; 2 wings; 3 landing gear; 4 engine; 5 tail.
6. Listening: gist A; details 1 A; 2 A; 3 A; 4 A.

**Speaking rubric (8 points):** correct part names 3; correct basic functions 2; location language 1; clear sequence 1; scope/safety awareness 1.

**Independent work:** write a 100-word description of a conventional aircraft using at least six terms from the vocabulary table and four location phrases.

# Lesson 8. Materials Used in Aircraft Structures

**Suggested time:** 90 minutes

## Learning outcomes

Students will:
1. understand the difference between a material family and a specific alloy or composite;
2. use vocabulary for strength, density, corrosion resistance, fatigue and properties;
3. compare materials using comparative forms and cautious language;
4. read a simplified materials-selection note;
5. listen to a discussion about selecting a material for a hypothetical component;
6. explain why material choice depends on requirements and approved specifications.

## 1. Lead-in

Discuss:
- Why are different materials used in different parts of a product?
- Is the lightest material always the best choice?
- Why do cost, manufacturability and service environment matter?
- Which material properties are important in aircraft structures?

## 2. Vocabulary

| English | Russian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| material | материал | Material selection depends on requirements. |
| alloy | сплав | An alloy contains a metal and other element(s). |
| aluminium alloy | алюминиевый сплав | Some aluminium alloys are used in aircraft structures. |
| titanium alloy | титановый сплав | Titanium alloys are used in selected demanding applications. |
| composite material | композиционный материал | A composite combines different constituents. |
| fibre | волокно | Fibres can reinforce a composite. |
| matrix | матрица | The matrix holds the reinforcement together. |
| density | плотность | Density is mass per unit volume. |
| strength | прочность | Strength describes resistance to applied stress. |
| stiffness | жёсткость | Stiffness describes resistance to elastic deformation. |
| fatigue | усталость материала | Fatigue can develop under repeated loading. |
| corrosion resistance | коррозионная стойкость | Corrosion resistance matters in service environments. |
| fracture toughness | вязкость разрушения | Fracture toughness relates to resistance to crack growth. |
| manufacturability | технологичность изготовления | Manufacturability affects production choices. |
| specification | спецификация | The specification defines required properties. |

### Material families

- metals and alloys;
- polymers;
- ceramics;
- composites.

A material family is not a complete engineering specification. Properties vary by grade, treatment, product form, processing history and test conditions.

## 3. Reading: a simplified materials-selection note

Read:

“Material selection for an aircraft component is a multi-factor decision. Designers and materials specialists consider mechanical properties, density, corrosion behaviour, temperature range, fatigue performance, manufacturing methods, inspection requirements, availability and cost. Aluminium alloys are used in many structural applications because they can offer a useful combination of low density, strength and manufacturability. Titanium alloys are selected for some applications where their properties justify their higher cost and more demanding processing. Fibre-reinforced composites can provide high specific strength and stiffness, but their design, manufacture and inspection require specialised knowledge. No material is suitable for every component. The approved design specification defines what is acceptable.”

### First reading — gist

A) Material selection balances several properties and production requirements.  
B) One material is best for every aircraft component.  
C) Material selection depends only on colour.

### Second reading — details

1. Name four factors considered during material selection.
2. Why are aluminium alloys used in many structural applications?
3. Why might titanium alloys be selected only for some applications?
4. What is one advantage of fibre-reinforced composites?
5. Why do composites require specialised knowledge?
6. What defines acceptable material requirements?

### Critical thinking

Avoid absolute statements such as “titanium is always stronger” or “composites are always lighter and better”. A material comparison must specify the grade, form, loading, environment and design requirement. Students must not select real aircraft materials independently; use the approved specification in professional practice.

## 4. Language focus: comparisons and cautious claims

- **lighter than:** Material A is lighter than Material B.
- **more resistant to:** This alloy may be more resistant to corrosion in a specified environment.
- **less expensive than:** The material is less expensive than the alternative.
- **as strong as:** The tested sample is as strong as the reference sample under the stated conditions.
- **can offer / may provide:** Composite materials can offer high specific stiffness.
- **depends on:** The choice depends on the design requirements.

### Practice 1. Complete with comparative forms

1. This material is (light) ______ than the reference material.
2. The new process is (expensive) ______ than the previous one.
3. The specification is (important) ______ than a general preference.
4. This option may be (suitable) ______ for the stated conditions.
5. No single material is (good) ______ for every application.

## 5. Listening: discussing a hypothetical material choice

**Before you listen**

Words: *density, strength, corrosion, manufacturing, specification, requirement*. Predict which questions a materials specialist might ask before comparing two materials.

**First listening — gist**

What are the speakers doing?

A) Comparing two hypothetical material options against requirements.  
B) Ordering lunch.  
C) Planning a holiday.

**Second listening — details**

1. What is the component for?
   A) a classroom design exercise  
   B) an actual aircraft repair  
   C) a household decoration
2. Which properties do they compare?
   A) density and corrosion behaviour  
   B) colour and packaging  
   C) brand and advertising
3. What does the instructor say is essential?
   A) the approved specification  
   B) personal preference  
   C) the cheapest price alone
4. What conclusion do they reach?
   A) they need the full requirements before choosing  
   B) the lightest option is always correct  
   C) they can select a real aircraft material without approval

**Recording script**

**Student:** We have two material options for our classroom design exercise. Can we choose the lighter one?

**Instructor:** Not from density alone. What requirements does the component have?

**Student:** We need to compare strength, corrosion behaviour and manufacturing requirements.

**Instructor:** Correct. We also need the product form, operating environment and approved specification.

**Student:** One option may be easier to machine, but the other may have different performance characteristics.

**Instructor:** Exactly. A material choice is a balance. For a real aircraft component, only the approved design and material documentation can define the acceptable option.

**Student:** So our classroom conclusion should be conditional: we need more information before recommending one material.

**Instructor:** Yes. Explain which data is missing.

**Language focus**
- *not from density alone* — rejecting an oversimplified criterion;
- *may be easier to machine* — cautious comparison;
- *depends on the requirements* — conditional reasoning;
- *before recommending* — sequencing.

**After listening**

Complete a comparison table for two fictional materials using data supplied by the teacher. If no data is provided, list the properties that must be checked rather than inventing values.

## 6. Speaking and writing

Write 100–120 words answering: “Why is material selection a multi-factor decision?” Include:
- three material properties;
- one manufacturing consideration;
- one comparison;
- one cautious phrase (*may, can, depends on*);
- a final reference to the specification.

## 7. Self-check and teacher key

1. What is an alloy?

3. Why is density alone insufficient for material selection?
4. Practice 1: 1 lighter; 2 less/more expensive (depending on intended comparison; specify the meaning); 3 more important; 4 more suitable; 5 good (rewrite as “No single material is best for every application” for a natural comparative-free form).
5. Listening: gist A; details 1 A; 2 A; 3 A; 4 A.

**Writing rubric (8 points):** multi-factor explanation 2; correct property vocabulary 2; comparison 1; cautious claim 1; specification reference 1; clarity 1.

**Independent work:** create a glossary card for five material terms. Give a simple definition and one example sentence for each.

## Teacher notes and implementation boundary

- These are original draft lessons 6–8 for the third course, first semester. The existing Technical 1.1–1.3 and 2.1–2.2 remain unchanged.
- The aviation content is an introductory ESP bridge. It is not an approved maintenance manual, engineering specification, manufacturing instruction or certification guide.

- The four professional strands are represented: technologists (process sheets/materials), programmers (digital production data), electricians (electrical documentation coordination), machine-tool operators (dimensions and manufactured components).
- Listening scripts are texts for future recording, not finished audio.
- Validate technical terminology and learning hours against current programme documents and specialist references before publication.
