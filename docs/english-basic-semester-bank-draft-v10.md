# Basic English — Semester Assessment Bank (Draft v10, Questions 181–200)

Статус: самостоятельный авторский черновик для методической проверки. Не импортировать в защищённый банк до сверки с рабочей программой и точным изданием учебника Белова.

Ориентир: A2–B1. Темы: путешествия, окружающая среда, цифровая коммуникация, планы и чтение. Один предполагаемый правильный ответ на вопрос.

## Questions

**181.** We ___ our hotel online before we travelled to the city.
- A. book
- B. have booked
- C. had booked
- D. are booking

**182.** You will miss the beginning of the film unless you ___ now.
- A. leave
- B. will leave
- C. left
- D. leaving

**183.** The guide told us ___ close to the group during the excursion.
- A. stay
- B. staying
- C. stayed
- D. to stay

**184.** There are not ___ recycling bins in this part of the town.
- A. much
- B. enough
- C. little
- D. any of

**185.** My train was delayed, ___ I arrived later than planned.
- A. because
- B. although
- C. so
- D. unless

**186.** The woman ___ helped us find the museum was very friendly.
- A. which
- B. whose
- C. where
- D. who

**187.** I ___ my passport yet, so I cannot complete the online check-in.
- A. haven't found
- B. didn't found
- C. don't found
- D. wasn't find

**188.** This route is ___ than the one we took yesterday.
- A. more short
- B. shortest
- C. shorter
- D. the shorter

**189.** You ___ share your account password with other people.
- A. don't have
- B. mustn't
- C. should
- D. may

**190.** The phrase *to protect the environment* means:
- A. to use more disposable products
- B. to ignore pollution
- C. to increase unnecessary waste
- D. to take action to keep the natural world safe

### Read the text and answer Questions 191–195.

*A small town library started a project to help residents use digital public services. Volunteers showed visitors how to create secure passwords, recognise suspicious messages and find official government websites. The sessions were free, and visitors could bring their own phones or use library computers. The organisers explained that participants should never send personal details in response to an unexpected message. They also advised people to check the website address carefully before entering information. At the end of the project, the library published a simple guide that residents could take home.*

**191.** What was the aim of the library project?
- A. To teach residents to use digital public services safely
- B. To sell new computers to visitors
- C. To replace all local government offices
- D. To provide paid language lessons

**192.** Which topic was included in the sessions?
- A. How to repair a car engine
- B. How to recognise suspicious messages
- C. How to write a travel diary
- D. How to design a library building

**193.** What could visitors use during the sessions?
- A. Only the volunteers' personal phones
- B. Only printed forms
- C. Their own phones or library computers
- D. Computers in a different town

**194.** What did organisers advise people not to do?
- A. Visit the library
- B. Read a simple guide
- C. Check website addresses
- D. Send personal details in response to an unexpected message

**195.** What happened at the end of the project?
- A. The library closed permanently.
- B. The library published a guide for residents.
- C. All participants received new phones.
- D. The volunteers deleted the official websites.

**196.** If I ___ more time next weekend, I will visit the science museum.
- A. have
- B. had
- C. will have
- D. having

**197.** The email ___ by the manager before it was sent to the client.
- A. checks
- B. has checked
- C. had been checked
- D. is checking

**198.** Choose the most appropriate response to “I'm afraid I can't attend the meeting tomorrow.”
- A. You are afraid tomorrow.
- B. It attends you.
- C. No meeting is tomorrow yesterday.
- D. That's a pity. Would you like me to send you the notes?

**199.** In the text, the word *suspicious* is closest in meaning to:
- A. easy to understand
- B. likely to be dishonest or unsafe
- C. officially approved
- D. free of charge

**200.** The volunteers explained the instructions slowly ___ everyone could follow them.
- A. in spite of
- B. unless
- C. so that
- D. whereas

## Teacher key and rationale

| No. | Key | Rationale |
|---:|:---:|---|
| 181 | C | Past perfect shows the booking was completed before the later past event of travelling. |
| 182 | A | In a first conditional clause after “unless”, use present simple. |
| 183 | D | “Tell someone to do something” takes the to-infinitive. |
| 184 | B | “Enough” means the required quantity; “bins” is countable plural. |
| 185 | C | “So” links the delay to its result. |
| 186 | D | “Who” is the relative pronoun for a person as the subject. |
| 187 | A | Present perfect with “yet” is used for an unfinished situation up to now. |
| 188 | C | The comparative form of “short” is “shorter”. |
| 189 | B | “Mustn't” expresses prohibition. |
| 190 | D | Protecting the environment means taking steps to keep nature safe. |
| 191 | A | The opening sentence states that the project helped residents use digital public services. |
| 192 | B | The text explicitly lists recognising suspicious messages. |
| 193 | C | Visitors could bring their own phones or use library computers. |
| 194 | D | The organisers warned against sending personal details in response to unexpected messages. |
| 195 | B | The library published a simple guide at the end. |
| 196 | A | In the first conditional, the if-clause uses present simple. |
| 197 | C | Past perfect passive shows the email was checked before it was sent. |
| 198 | D | This response acknowledges the problem and offers a useful alternative. |
| 199 | B | “Suspicious” describes something that may be dishonest or unsafe. |
| 200 | C | “So that” introduces the intended purpose. |
