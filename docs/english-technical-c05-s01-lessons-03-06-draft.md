# Lesson 3. Maintenance Documentation and Technical Manuals

**ID:** `en-c05-s01-l03`

### Learning outcomes
Students can explain the purpose of technical documentation; distinguish a manual, work card, logbook, service bulletin and revision record at a general language level; locate key information in a fictional document extract; and summarize a document status in clear English.

### 1. Lead-in
Discuss: Why do technical teams need written records? What problems can occur when two people use different document revisions? Which documents might be relevant to electricians, programmers, technologists and CNC operators?

### 2. Vocabulary

| English | Russian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| technical manual | техническое руководство | The manual describes the system at a general level. |
| maintenance record | запись о техническом обслуживании | The record identifies the document used. |
| work card | рабочая карта | A work card may define an approved task. |
| logbook | журнал учёта | The logbook contains dated entries. |
| service bulletin | сервисный бюллетень | A bulletin communicates specified information. |
| revision | редакция, версия документа | Check the revision identifier. |
| revision history | история изменений | The revision history lists changes. |
| issue date | дата выпуска | The issue date is shown in the header. |
| effective date | дата вступления в силу | The effective date may differ from the issue date. |
| applicability | применимость | Applicability defines where a document applies. |
| reference number | номер документа / ссылки | Record the reference number accurately. |
| superseded | заменённый более новой редакцией | The old revision is marked as superseded. |
| controlled copy | контролируемая копия | Use the controlled copy supplied by the organization. |
| traceability | прослеживаемость | Traceability links a record to its source. |

### 3. Reading: Why document control matters

Technical work depends on reliable information. A document may describe a system, define a task, record an inspection or communicate a change. The title alone is not enough to identify the correct document. A reader may also need its reference number, revision, issue date, effective date and applicability statement.

A revision history explains how a document has changed. A newer revision may replace an older one, but a reader must not assume that every document applies to every product or situation. Applicability is determined by the controlled documentation and the organization’s procedures.

Traceability means that a record can be connected to the information and activity it describes. A clear record identifies its source and avoids vague references such as “the latest file” or “the usual drawing”. This is important in every engineering role. A technologist may refer to a process sheet; a programmer may identify a software specification; an electrician may read a controlled system description; a CNC operator may need the correct drawing revision for a classroom exercise.

**Fictional document header**

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Title | Training Component Inspection Record |
| Reference | TRN-QA-014 |
| Revision | C |
| Issue date | 12 March 2026 |
| Status | Classroom example |
| Applicability | Fictional training component only |
| Supersedes | Revision B |

**Comprehension**
1. Why is a title alone insufficient?
2. What does a revision history explain?
3. What does applicability define?
4. What is traceability?
5. Which fields in the sample identify the document version and scope?

### 4. Listening: Document-control briefing

**Before you listen:** Predict the meaning of *reference, revision, superseded, applicability, traceability*.

**First listening — gist.** Choose the main idea:
A. A team explains how to identify a controlled classroom document.
B. A team gives instructions for repairing an aircraft.
C. A team selects a new supplier.

**Second listening — details**
1. What is the document reference?
2. Which revision is current in the fictional example?
3. What does the status field say?
4. What does the applicability field limit?
5. What should a student do if the revision is unclear?

**Recording script**

“Before you use this training record, check the document header. The reference number is TRN-QA-014 and the revision is C. Revision B is listed as superseded in this fictional example. The status field says ‘classroom example’, and the applicability field limits the document to a fictional training component. If a revision or applicability statement is unclear, do not guess. Ask the document owner or teacher to confirm which controlled copy is intended for the exercise. In a real organization, follow its approved document-control process.”

**Transcript reveal / key:** gist A; 1 TRN-QA-014; 2 C; 3 classroom example; 4 fictional training component only; 5 ask the document owner or teacher.

### 5. Language focus: Relative clauses and document references

Relative clauses add identifying information:
- *The document that contains the revision history is available in the training folder.*
- *The record, which is marked as a classroom example, is not an operational document.*

Use *that* for defining information and *which* for additional information after a comma.

Combine:
1. This is the record. It identifies the document revision.
2. The file is a classroom example. It must not be used as an operational instruction.
3. The revision history lists changes. It supports traceability.

**Suggested answers**
1. This is the record that identifies the document revision.
2. The file, which is a classroom example, must not be used as an operational instruction.
3. The revision history, which lists changes, supports traceability.

### 6. Guided practice

**A. Document scan.** Find the reference, revision, date, status and applicability in the sample header.

**B. Correct the vague statements**
- “I used the newest file.”
- “It is the normal drawing.”
- “The document is probably applicable.”

**Suggested improved wording**
- “I used document TRN-QA-014, revision C, as specified for this classroom task.”
- “I used the drawing identified by the reference and revision in the exercise brief.”
- “The applicability statement needs confirmation.”

**C. Four professional routes**
- Technologist: identify the revision of a fictional process sheet.
- Programmer: record the version of a sample software specification.
- Electrician: distinguish a system overview from an approved task document.
- CNC operator: identify the reference and revision of a training drawing.

### 7. Speaking and writing

In pairs, conduct a document handover: one student asks for the reference and revision; the other confirms the details and states any limitation.

Write a 100–130-word document-status note. Include title, reference, revision, status, applicability and one clarification request.

### 8. Self-check

1. A revision history records: a) document changes; b) staff lunch breaks; c) machine colour.
2. Applicability describes: a) where or to what the document applies; b) the font size; c) the author’s age.
3. Traceability connects a record to: a) its source and context; b) a random file; c) an unrelated task.
4. If the revision is unclear, you should: a) guess; b) ask the responsible person; c) use any available copy.
5. “Superseded” means: a) replaced by a later version; b) approved for every use; c) missing.

**Key:** 1a, 2a, 3a, 4b, 5a.

**Independent work:** Prepare a fictional controlled-document cover sheet with six metadata fields and a 70-word explanation of why revision control matters.

# Lesson 4. Fault Diagnosis: Symptoms, Evidence and Reports

**ID:** `en-c05-s01-l04`

### Learning outcomes
Students can distinguish a symptom, observation, fault hypothesis and confirmed cause; describe a problem without guessing; organize evidence in a report; and use cautious language to communicate uncertainty.

### 1. Lead-in
Discuss: Is “the system is broken” a useful technical report? What information would make a report more precise? Why should a symptom not be confused with a root cause?

### 2. Vocabulary

| English | Russian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| fault | неисправность, отказ | The report describes a reported fault. |
| symptom | признак, проявление | A symptom may have several possible causes. |
| observation | наблюдение | Record the observation before interpreting it. |
| evidence | доказательство, фактические данные | The conclusion must be supported by evidence. |
| fault hypothesis | гипотеза о неисправности | A hypothesis requires further checking. |
| root cause | первопричина | The root cause is not yet confirmed. |
| intermittent | периодически возникающий | The fictional symptom is intermittent. |
| discrepancy | расхождение, несоответствие | The report notes a discrepancy. |
| abnormal | ненормальный, необычный | The record contains an abnormal indication. |
| reported condition | заявленное состояние | The reported condition is described by the user. |
| reproduce a problem | воспроизвести проблему | The team could not reproduce the problem in the exercise. |
| isolate | локализовать, отделить | The case does not authorize fault isolation. |
| investigate | исследовать | The responsible team may investigate the evidence. |
| corrective action | корректирующее действие | A corrective action requires authorization. |
| unresolved | нерешённый, неустановленный | The cause remains unresolved. |

### 3. Reading: Facts before conclusions

A fault report should help another person understand what was reported, what was observed and what is still unknown. These are different categories.

A **reported condition** is what a person says happened. An **observation** is information recorded during an authorized review. **Evidence** may include a dated record, a measurement sheet, a status message or a photograph where permitted. A **fault hypothesis** is a possible explanation. A **root cause** is a conclusion supported by an appropriate investigation.

Consider the fictional statement: “A warning appeared twice in a classroom simulation.” This is more useful than “the computer is faulty”. The first sentence describes an event. It does not claim to know why it happened.

Good reports use neutral language:
- “The message appeared at 10:15 in the training log.”
- “The same entry is present twice.”
- “The available record does not identify the cause.”
- “Further review by the responsible person is required.”

Avoid:
- “The sensor is definitely damaged.”
- “The programmer made a mistake.”
- “The component must be replaced.”

Those statements may be unsupported. Across all four professional routes, the writer should identify the source, date, document revision, observed condition and remaining uncertainty. Actual diagnosis and corrective action belong to authorized personnel following approved procedures.

**Fictional classroom case**
- Reported condition: a status message appeared during a simulated task.
- Observation: two identical entries appear in the sample log.
- Evidence: training log TL-08, revision A.
- Hypothesis: a duplicate record may have been created.
- Confirmed cause: not established.

**Comprehension**
1. What is a reported condition?
2. How is an observation different from a hypothesis?
3. What evidence is mentioned in the case?
4. Has the root cause been established?
5. Which phrases communicate uncertainty responsibly?

### 4. Listening: Writing a neutral fault report

**Before you listen:** Review *reported condition, observation, evidence, hypothesis, unresolved*.

**First listening — gist:** What is the speaker doing?
A. Separating facts from assumptions in a fictional report.
B. Confirming a real component failure.
C. Giving repair instructions.

**Second listening — details**
1. What was reported?
2. What does the sample log show?
3. What is the possible hypothesis?
4. Is the cause confirmed?
5. What should the report say?

**Recording script**

“The reported condition is that a warning message appeared during the classroom simulation. In training log TL-08, revision A, we can see two identical entries. This is an observation from the supplied record. One possible hypothesis is that the entry was duplicated during data handling, but the available evidence does not confirm the cause. The report should identify the time, the source document and the repeated entries. It should also state that the cause remains unresolved. Do not recommend a repair or identify a failed component on the basis of this classroom example.”

**Key:** gist A; 1 a warning message appeared; 2 two identical entries; 3 a duplicate may have been created during data handling; 4 no; 5 record the time/source/entries and state that the cause is unresolved.

### 5. Language focus: Hedging and reported information

Use cautious expressions:
- *may / might / could* — possibility;
- *appears to / seems to* — cautious observation;
- *the available evidence suggests…* — tentative interpretation;
- *has not been confirmed* — no confirmed conclusion;
- *according to the report…* — attributed information.

Rewrite:
1. “The sensor is broken.” (evidence is insufficient)
2. “The data file caused the problem.” (only a duplicate is observed)
3. “The component failed.” (the report only says a warning appeared)

**Suggested answers**
1. “The sensor may require further review; the available evidence does not confirm a fault.”
2. “The duplicate entry may be related to data handling, but the cause has not been confirmed.”
3. “According to the report, a warning appeared during the exercise.”

### 6. Guided practice

**A. Classify each sentence**
1. “The operator reported a warning.”
2. “The log contains two identical lines.”
3. “A data-handling issue may have occurred.”
4. “The root cause is confirmed.”

**Key:** 1 reported condition; 2 observation; 3 hypothesis; 4 confirmed conclusion only if supported by evidence (not supported by the supplied case).

**B. Complete a report template**
- Source and revision:
- Date/time:
- Reported condition:
- Observed evidence:
- Possible explanation (if appropriate):
- What remains unknown:
- Person/team to whom the matter is referred:

**C. Four professional routes**
- Electrician: describe a fictional status indication without suggesting a real electrical test.
- Programmer: describe a duplicated event in a sample log.
- Technologist: report a mismatch between two fictional document fields.
- CNC operator: describe an incomplete classroom measurement record without proposing a machine adjustment.

### 7. Speaking and writing

Role-play a handover. Student A describes a reported condition. Student B asks: *What did you observe? Which record supports this? Is the cause confirmed?*

Write a 120–150-word neutral fault report using the template. Include at least three cautious expressions.

### 8. Self-check

1. A symptom is: a) a reported or observed sign; b) always the root cause; c) a corrective action.
2. Evidence should: a) support statements; b) be replaced by guesses; c) be hidden.
3. “The cause may be related to data handling” expresses: a) certainty; b) possibility; c) a command.
4. If the cause is not known, the report should: a) invent one; b) state that it remains unresolved; c) blame a colleague.
5. A corrective action should be: a) authorized and supported by the applicable process; b) guessed by any reader; c) included without evidence.

**Key:** 1a, 2a, 3b, 4b, 5a.

**Independent work:** Create a fictional fault-report card with one reported condition, two observations, one unconfirmed hypothesis and a clear statement of what remains unknown.

# Lesson 5. Electrical Troubleshooting Communication

**ID:** `en-c05-s01-l05`

### Learning outcomes
Students can use English to request clarification, repeat information, confirm a reference and hand over an unresolved issue; distinguish communication about troubleshooting from performing troubleshooting; and write a concise technical handover.

### 1. Lead-in
Discuss: Why can a misunderstanding be dangerous in technical work? What phrases help confirm that two people are discussing the same document or record? When should a person stop and ask for clarification?

### 2. Vocabulary and phrases

| English | Russian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| troubleshooting | поиск и анализ неисправности | The team is discussing troubleshooting terminology. |
| handover | передача информации / смены | The handover summarizes the current status. |
| clarify | уточнить | Could you clarify the document reference? |
| confirm | подтвердить | Please confirm the revision number. |
| repeat | повторить | Could you repeat the last part? |
| read back | повторить полученную информацию для подтверждения | I will read back the reference number. |
| discrepancy | расхождение | There is a discrepancy between the two records. |
| unresolved issue | нерешённый вопрос | The issue remains unresolved. |
| scope | область / границы задачи | Please confirm the scope of the request. |
| authorization | разрешение, полномочия | The task requires appropriate authorization. |
| stop and ask | остановиться и уточнить | Stop and ask if the instruction is unclear. |
| refer to | направить к / сослаться на | Refer the question to the responsible specialist. |

**Useful communication phrases**
- “Could you repeat the reference number, please?”
- “Do you mean revision B or revision C?”
- “Let me read that back to confirm.”
- “I understand the reported condition, but the cause is not confirmed.”
- “I am not authorized to carry out that task.”
- “The information is unclear. I will stop and ask the responsible person.”
- “Could you point me to the applicable approved document?”

### 3. Reading: Communication is part of technical competence

Technical communication should be precise, calm and traceable. When a person receives a report, they should confirm the identity of the document or record, clarify unclear terms and repeat important details when necessary. This reduces the risk of acting on a misunderstanding.

A read-back is a short repetition of important information so that both people can confirm they have understood it correctly. It may include a reference number, revision, date, reported condition or agreed next communication step. It does not replace an approved procedure or authorize a task.

A professional should also recognize the limits of their role. If the information is incomplete, the document is unclear or the requested action is outside their authorization, the correct communication may be to stop and ask. This applies to electricians discussing system records, programmers reviewing software messages, technologists handling process documentation and CNC operators checking drawing references. Clear language is not a substitute for competence, supervision or approved technical instructions; it helps people communicate within those boundaries.

### 4. Listening: A handover with clarification

**Before you listen:** Predict which details may need a read-back: document number, revision, date, reported condition, unresolved issue.

**First listening — gist:** What is the purpose?
A. To confirm a fictional record and hand over an unresolved question.
B. To perform an electrical test.
C. To authorize a repair.

**Second listening — details**
1. What is the document reference?
2. Which revision is mentioned?
3. What discrepancy was reported?
4. Has the cause been confirmed?
5. What does the second speaker do to confirm the information?

**Recording script**

“Could you give me the document reference for the handover?”  
“It is TRN-EL-022, revision B.”  
“Let me read that back: TRN-EL-022, revision B. Is that correct?”  
“Yes. The classroom record shows a difference between two status descriptions.”  
“Has the cause been confirmed?”  
“No. We have only recorded the discrepancy. The source information still needs to be reviewed by the responsible teacher.”  
“Understood. I will note that the issue remains unresolved and will not make assumptions about the cause.”  
“Thank you. Please include the date and the reference in the handover note.”

**Key:** gist A; 1 TRN-EL-022; 2 B; 3 two status descriptions differ; 4 no; 5 repeats the reference and revision for confirmation.

### 5. Language focus: Polite questions and indirect questions

Direct questions can sound abrupt. Polite requests are useful in a professional handover:
- Direct: “What is the revision?”
- Polite: “Could you tell me what the revision is?”
- Direct: “Is the issue confirmed?”
- Polite: “Could you confirm whether the issue has been confirmed?”

In indirect questions, use statement word order:
- Correct: *Could you tell me where the record is?*
- Incorrect: *Could you tell me where is the record?*

Rewrite politely:
1. “Repeat the number.”
2. “What does this abbreviation mean?”
3. “Is this the current revision?”
4. “Who is responsible for this record?”

**Suggested answers**
1. “Could you repeat the number, please?”
2. “Could you explain what this abbreviation means?”
3. “Could you confirm whether this is the current revision?”
4. “Could you tell me who is responsible for this record?”

### 6. Guided practice

**A. Read-back drill.** In pairs, Student A reads a fictional reference and revision; Student B repeats them and asks for confirmation. Use invented identifiers only.

**B. Choose the professional response**
1. You cannot understand a task description.  
   a) Guess. b) Stop and ask for clarification. c) Ask an unauthorized person to do it.
2. You are asked to carry out a task outside your authorization.  
   a) Explain the limitation and refer the request to the responsible person. b) Try it anyway. c) Ignore the record.
3. Two documents show different revisions.  
   a) Choose one randomly. b) Record the discrepancy and request confirmation. c) Delete one copy.

**Key:** 1b, 2a, 3b.

**C. Four professional routes**
- Electrician: clarify which fictional system description is being discussed.
- Programmer: read back a sample error-code identifier.
- Technologist: confirm a process-sheet revision.
- CNC operator: confirm the drawing reference used in a classroom exercise.

### 7. Speaking and writing

**Speaking:** Perform a two-minute handover. Include a polite clarification request, a read-back, a statement of uncertainty and a clear next communication step.

**Writing:** Write a 100–130-word handover note. Identify the source, reference, revision, reported issue, what is known, what is not known and who should clarify it.

### 8. Self-check

1. A read-back is used to: a) confirm important information; b) replace technical approval; c) change a document.
2. Which is a polite clarification request? a) “Explain now.” b) “Could you clarify the reference, please?” c) “I will guess.”
3. Indirect question word order is: a) statement order; b) always question order; c) random.
4. If a task is outside your authorization, you should: a) proceed; b) explain the limitation and refer it appropriately; c) hide the request.
5. A handover should distinguish: a) facts and unresolved issues; b) facts and guesses as if they were equal; c) unrelated opinions.

**Key:** 1a, 2b, 3a, 4b, 5a.

**Independent work:** Record a short written dialogue (10–12 lines) in which two colleagues clarify a document reference and hand over an unresolved fictional issue. Include at least four polite phrases.

# Lesson 6. Quality Assurance and Non-conformity Reports

**ID:** `en-c05-s01-l06`

### Learning outcomes
Students can explain quality assurance and quality control at an introductory level; distinguish a requirement, result, deviation and non-conformity; describe a fictional inspection record; and write a clear non-conformity statement without inventing causes or acceptance criteria.

### 1. Lead-in
Discuss: What is quality in engineering? Why must an inspection result be compared with a stated requirement? Is every difference automatically a non-conformity?

### 2. Vocabulary

| English | Russian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| quality assurance (QA) | обеспечение качества | QA focuses on planned processes that support quality. |
| quality control (QC) | контроль качества | QC includes checking outputs against requirements. |
| requirement | требование | The requirement must be identified. |
| specification | спецификация | The specification states defined characteristics. |
| inspection | проверка, контроль | The inspection record contains a result. |
| acceptance criterion | критерий приёмки | Use only the criterion stated in the approved source. |
| deviation | отклонение | A deviation is a difference from a defined reference. |
| non-conformity | несоответствие | The report describes a non-conformity. |
| corrective action | корректирующее действие | Corrective action addresses an identified issue. |
| preventive action | предупреждающее действие | Preventive action aims to reduce recurrence. |
| audit | аудит | An audit reviews evidence and processes. |
| evidence record | запись с подтверждающими данными | Keep the evidence record traceable. |
| disposition | решение о дальнейшей судьбе несоответствующего объекта | Disposition is made by authorized personnel. |
| verify | проверять, подтверждать | Verify the record against its source. |

### 3. Reading: QA, QC and non-conformity

Quality assurance and quality control are related but different. Quality assurance focuses on the processes and arrangements intended to support consistent quality. Quality control focuses on checking whether a product, service or result meets specified requirements.

A requirement must come from an appropriate source. It may be stated in a specification, drawing, contract, process document or other controlled reference. A result cannot be judged correctly if the relevant requirement is unknown or the document revision is unclear.

A non-conformity is a failure to meet a specified requirement. A difference is not automatically a non-conformity until it has been compared with the applicable requirement. A non-conformity report should identify the item or record, the requirement, the observed result and the evidence. It should avoid unsupported explanations.

A corrective action addresses an identified non-conformity or its cause. A preventive action aims to reduce the likelihood of a potential problem. Decisions about disposition and corrective action must be made by authorized personnel under the relevant quality system.

In the four professional routes, quality information may include process records, software test results, electrical documentation or measurement sheets. The language pattern is the same: identify the reference, state the evidence, describe the difference and refer the issue to the responsible role.

**Fictional classroom example — not a real acceptance standard**

| Field | Training example |
|---|---|
| Item | Sample part P-04 |
| Reference | Classroom drawing DR-04, revision A |
| Requirement | Feature label must be present |
| Observation | Feature label is missing from the sample record |
| Evidence | Training inspection sheet QF-06 |
| Status | For teacher review; no disposition assigned |

**Comprehension**
1. What is the main focus of quality assurance?
2. What does quality control check?
3. Why must the applicable requirement be identified?
4. What is a non-conformity?
5. Who should decide the disposition of a real non-conforming item?

### 4. Listening: Reporting a classroom non-conformity

**Before you listen:** Review *requirement, observation, evidence, deviation, disposition*.

**First listening — gist:** What is the speaker doing?
A. Describing a documented difference between a requirement and a sample record.
B. Approving a real aircraft component.
C. Giving instructions for changing a machine setting.

**Second listening — details**
1. What is the sample item?
2. Which reference is used?
3. What is missing?
4. Which evidence record is mentioned?
5. Has a disposition been assigned?

**Recording script**

“This is a classroom non-conformity example for sample part P-04. The reference is drawing DR-04, revision A. The stated training requirement is that the feature label must be present. The inspection sheet QF-06 shows that the label is missing from the sample record. The report should identify the requirement, the observation and the evidence. No disposition has been assigned in this exercise. The case is for learning how to write a factual report; it does not define a real acceptance criterion or authorize a production decision.”

**Key:** gist A; 1 sample part P-04; 2 DR-04, revision A; 3 the feature label; 4 QF-06; 5 no.

### 5. Language focus: Comparing requirements and results

Useful patterns:
- *The requirement states that…*
- *The record shows that…*
- *The observed result differs from…*
- *The evidence indicates that…*
- *The cause has not been established.*
- *The item is referred for review.*

Use *whereas* and *while* to contrast:
- *The requirement states that the label must be present, whereas the sample record shows no label.*
- *The drawing identifies revision A, while the inspection sheet refers to revision B.*

Complete:
1. The requirement ______ that the label must be present. (state)
2. The inspection sheet ______ the missing label. (show)
3. The cause ______ not been established. (have)
4. The report identifies the requirement, ______ the evidence records the observation. (whereas / because)

**Key:** 1 states; 2 shows; 3 has; 4 whereas.

**A. Requirement or observation?**
1. “The label must be present.”
2. “The label is missing from the sample record.”
3. “The drawing reference is DR-04, revision A.”
4. “The missing label was caused by an operator error.”

**Key:** 1 requirement; 2 observation; 3 reference information; 4 unsupported cause unless separately evidenced.

- Programmer: report a failed test case against a stated classroom requirement.
- Electrician: report a mismatch between two fictional document labels, not a real circuit condition.
- CNC operator: report a missing identifier in a training measurement record, not a real machining defect.

### 7. Speaking and writing

**Speaking:** Present a one-minute non-conformity summary. State the requirement first, then the observation and evidence. Finish by identifying what remains unknown.

**Writing:** Write a 120–150-word non-conformity report. Use neutral wording and do not invent acceptance limits, root causes or corrective actions.

### 8. Self-check

1. Quality assurance focuses mainly on: a) planned processes; b) guessing inspection results; c) changing labels.
2. Quality control checks: a) results against requirements; b) personal preferences; c) unrelated documents.
3. A non-conformity is: a) failure to meet a specified requirement; b) every small difference; c) a maintenance task.
4. A requirement must be: a) identified from an appropriate source; b) invented after inspection; c) assumed.
5. If the cause is unknown, the report should: a) state that it is not established; b) assign blame; c) invent a cause.

**Key:** 1a, 2a, 3a, 4a, 5a.

**Independent work:** Create a fictional quality report with one requirement, one observed difference and two pieces of traceable evidence. Add a 70–90-word explanation of why the cause cannot be assumed.

### B. Language check
Choose the best option:
1. Could you tell me where the document ______? (is / is it)
2. The cause ______ not been confirmed. (has / have)
3. The record ______ the observation. (shows / show)
4. The issue may ______ related to a duplicate entry. (be / is)
5. The requirement states ______ the label must be present. (that / what)

**Key:** 1 is; 2 has; 3 shows; 4 be; 5 that.

### C. Integrated listening — Quality handover

**First listening — gist:** A colleague hands over a fictional document discrepancy and identifies the information still needed.

**Second listening — details**
1. Which document reference is mentioned?
2. What difference appears in the records?
3. Is the cause known?
4. What should the next reviewer confirm?

**Recording script**

“The handover concerns training record QA-17. The process sheet is revision B, while the attached classroom summary refers to revision A. This is a document discrepancy. We have not established why the two revisions differ. Please confirm the intended source document with the teacher before continuing the exercise. In the report, identify both references, state that the cause is unknown and keep the original records unchanged. This is a classroom document-control task, not a production decision.”

**Key:** 1 QA-17; 2 process sheet revision B versus summary revision A; 3 no; 4 the intended source document.

### D. Integrated writing task
Write a 160–180-word professional handover that includes:
- document reference and revision;
- reported condition;
- two observations;
- one unresolved question;
- the evidence available;
- the person or role to whom clarification is referred.

**Assessment rubric (10 points)**
- accurate use of technical vocabulary — 0–2;
- clear distinction between facts and assumptions — 0–2;
- traceable references — 0–2;
- grammar and professional register — 0–2;
- clear next communication step — 0–2.

### Teacher notes
- Accept equivalent accurate language.
- Assess the student’s ability to separate a reported condition, observation, hypothesis and confirmed cause.
- Do not treat classroom examples as real maintenance instructions, inspection criteria or authorization.
- Do not award credit for invented causes or unsupported corrective actions.
- Listening scripts are original text for teacher reading or future recording; they are not MP3 files.
