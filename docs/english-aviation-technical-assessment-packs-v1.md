# Aviation-focused Technical English — assessment packs D and E

Status: new supplementary content. These packs are additive: they do not replace existing lectures, semester checks, examination banks, or the working Basic/Technical VPR exams.

## Scope and professional pathways

All reading passages and task contexts are aviation-related. The language is shared across the four college pathways, while the situations deliberately rotate between:
- **Manufacturing technologists:** airframe materials, process sheets, production sequence, inspection and quality records.
- **Programmers:** aircraft/maintenance information systems, sensor data, software validation, structured logs and algorithms.
- **Electricians:** aircraft electrical power, wiring, connectors, continuity, voltage checks and fault isolation.
- **Machine-tool operators:** CNC machining of aircraft components, engineering drawings, tolerances, tool offsets and dimensional inspection.

These are English-language learning scenarios, not maintenance instructions. Any practical work on real aircraft must follow approved technical documentation, authorisations and safety procedures.

## Pack D — Aircraft structures, manufacturing and quality

Suggested level: A2+/B1. Suggested time: 25–30 minutes. 20 items, 1 point each.
Task formats: multiple choice, matching, sequencing, gap completion and reading comprehension.

### Reading text

**From material to aircraft component**

An aircraft manufacturer receives aluminium alloy sheets and titanium billets from approved suppliers. Each delivery arrives with a batch number and a material certificate. The receiving team checks the documents before the material is released to production.

A technologist prepares a process sheet for each component. It lists the drawing number, material grade, operations, inspection points and revision level. A CNC operator uses the approved drawing and setup sheet to machine a bracket. The operator checks the tool offset and the workpiece position before the production run. After machining, an inspector measures the critical dimensions and records the results.

If a measurement is outside the specified tolerance, the part is identified and moved to a controlled non-conforming area. It is not sent to assembly. The quality engineer reviews the measurement record and decides what action is permitted under the quality procedure. Traceability is essential: the company must be able to identify the material batch, machine, process revision and inspection result for every component.

## Pack E — Aircraft electrical systems, digital diagnostics and CNC data

Suggested level: B1. Suggested time: 25–30 minutes. 20 items, 1 point each.
Task formats: multiple choice, matching, sequencing, gap completion and reading comprehension.

### Reading text

**Electrical fault reporting**

During a scheduled inspection of an aircraft training rig, a technician reports that one indicator does not illuminate. The electrical team first checks the maintenance record and confirms the equipment identification. They then consult the approved wiring diagram to understand the circuit layout.

A digital multimeter is used by authorised personnel in accordance with the training procedure. The recorded readings are compared with the specified values in the task documentation. The technician does not replace a component simply because it is suspected. The team records the symptom, test conditions, measured values and equipment identification. If the result is unclear, the issue is referred to the instructor or responsible engineer.

A programmer working on the training database checks that each fault report has a unique ID, a timestamp and a valid equipment code. A validation rule rejects incomplete records. The dashboard groups reports by system and status, helping the team identify recurring issues. The database supports analysis; it does not replace approved technical documentation or professional authorisation.

### Reading text 2

**CNC production data for an aircraft panel bracket**

A machine-tool operator receives a released job package for a small aircraft panel bracket. The package includes a drawing, a material specification, a tool list and a CNC program revision. Before machining, the operator checks that the program revision matches the released job package.

The first component is treated as a first-off inspection item. Its dimensions are checked and the results are recorded before the batch continues. The programmer reviews the simulation output and checks the toolpath for unexpected movement. The technologist confirms that the selected material and process route match the production specification. If any document has a different revision, the team stops the release process and requests clarification through the document-control system.
