# Technical English — Course 4, Semester 1 — Lessons 10–12
## Aircraft electrical systems: power distribution, wiring documentation and fault reporting

**Status:** teaching-material draft for editorial review.  
**Level:** Technical English / ESP, fourth year.  
**Audience:** students preparing for work with aircraft electrical systems, electrical equipment, manufacturing and maintenance documentation.  
**Safety boundary:** all examples are language-learning simulations. They are not instructions for testing, repairing, energising or modifying real aircraft systems. Real work requires approved maintenance data, authorised personnel and the applicable safety procedures.

## Block outcomes

By the end of the block, students can:
- identify the main sources and distribution stages of electrical power in a simplified aircraft system;
- understand basic labels and symbols in a training wiring diagram;
- distinguish a connector, a bonding connection and a ground point in technical descriptions;
- report a measurement and describe an observed fault without guessing its cause;
- extract key information from a short spoken technical briefing.

# Lesson 10. Aircraft Electrical Power: Sources and Distribution

**Duration:** 90 minutes

## Learning outcomes
Students can name common aircraft electrical power sources, describe a simplified distribution path, use sequence language and distinguish a normal operating description from a fault report.

## 1. Lead-in
Discuss:
1. Why does an aircraft need electrical power?
2. Which systems may depend on electrical power?
3. Why is reliable power distribution important?

## 2. Key vocabulary

| English | Russian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| electrical power | электрическая энергия | Electrical power is distributed to several systems. |
| power source | источник питания | The training diagram shows two power sources. |
| generator | генератор | A generator supplies electrical power. |
| battery | аккумуляторная батарея | The battery is shown as a separate source. |
| busbar / bus | шина | The bus connects a source with distribution circuits. |
| distribution | распределение | Power distribution is shown in the diagram. |
| circuit breaker | автоматический выключатель | The circuit breaker protects the circuit. |
| load | нагрузка / потребитель | Each load is represented by a symbol. |
| backup supply | резервное питание | A backup supply is available in the simplified example. |
| supply voltage | напряжение питания | The specified supply voltage is given in the document. |

Pronunciation focus: *generator* /ˈdʒenəreɪtə(r)/; *battery* /ˈbætəri/; *distribution* /ˌdɪstrɪˈbjuːʃən/.

## 3. Language focus: describing a system
Use the Present Simple for general functions:
- A generator supplies electrical power.
- The bus distributes power to selected loads.
- A protective device opens the circuit when its specified conditions are met.

Use sequence markers to explain a diagram:
**first → then → after that → finally**.

Model:
“First, the source provides electrical power. Then, the power is routed through the distribution system. After that, the relevant circuits supply the connected loads. The diagram also identifies protective devices.”

## 4. Reading: A simplified training diagram

A training aircraft electrical-system diagram shows a generator, a battery, a distribution bus, protective devices and several electrical loads. The generator is represented as one source of electrical power. The battery is shown as a separate source. Both are connected to the distribution arrangement in the simplified drawing. The bus provides a common distribution point. Individual circuits lead from the distribution area to the loads.

The diagram is not a maintenance procedure. It does not provide switching instructions, operating limits or test points. Its purpose is to help a student recognise components and understand the relationship between a source, a distribution point and a load.

### Check your understanding
1. Name two sources shown in the text.
2. What is the function of the bus in this simplified description?
3. Does the training diagram provide instructions for operating an aircraft system? Explain.
4. Find one sentence that describes a general function.

## 5. Listening: Electrical power overview

**Before listening:** predict three words you may hear: *source, bus, load, generator, battery*.

**Audio script for browser speech synthesis:**

“Good morning. This is a classroom overview of a simplified aircraft electrical system. The diagram identifies a generator and a battery as two electrical power sources. The sources are connected to a distribution arrangement. A bus provides a common point from which several circuits are represented. Each circuit supplies a particular load. Protective devices are included in the drawing. Please remember that this diagram is for vocabulary and system-description practice only. It is not an approved maintenance document.”

**First listening — gist:** What is the speaker describing?
A. A hydraulic system  
B. A simplified electrical power system  
C. A passenger boarding procedure

**Second listening — details:**
1. Which two sources are named?
2. What does the bus provide?
3. What is the purpose of the protective devices in the drawing?
4. Is the diagram an approved maintenance document?

## 6. Practice
**A. Match the term and definition.**
1. source
2. bus
3. load
4. protective device

a. a component or system that uses electrical power  
b. an element that helps protect a circuit  
c. a point or arrangement used to distribute power  
d. where electrical power originates

**B. Complete the description.** Use: *source, bus, load, finally*.
“First, the ______ provides power. Then, power is routed to the distribution ______. The circuit supplies the connected ______. ______, the diagram shows the protective devices.”

**C. Speaking.** Describe the simplified diagram in 5–6 sentences. Do not add components that are not named.

## 7. Self-check — answers
Listening: 1-B; 2-generator and battery; 3-a common distribution point; 4-protective devices are included in the drawing; 5-no.  
Practice A: 1-d, 2-c, 3-a, 4-b.  
Practice B: source; bus; load; finally.

## 8. Independent work
Create a bilingual glossary of 12 terms from this lesson. Write a 100–120-word description of the simplified training diagram. Include at least four sequence markers. Do not write operational or maintenance instructions.

# Lesson 11. Wiring Diagrams, Connectors, Bonding and Grounding

**Duration:** 90 minutes

## Learning outcomes
Students can identify the purpose of a wiring diagram, recognise common drawing labels, distinguish connectors from bonding and grounding references, and describe a documented connection using cautious language.

## 1. Lead-in
Look at a simplified wiring diagram supplied by the teacher. Identify:
- a line representing a wire;
- a connector symbol;
- a component label;
- a reference or ground symbol;
- a drawing title and revision field.

Do not infer a real aircraft pin-out or connection procedure from a classroom illustration.

## 2. Key vocabulary

| English | Russian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| wiring diagram | схема электропроводки | The wiring diagram identifies the circuit connections. |
| wire | провод | The wire is labelled in the drawing. |
| harness | жгут проводов | The harness is represented as a grouped route. |
| connector | разъём | The connector is identified by a reference code. |
| pin | контакт разъёма | The drawing labels each pin position. |
| terminal | клемма / контактный вывод | The terminal is shown in the component view. |
| bonding | электрическое соединение для уравнивания потенциалов | Bonding is described in the approved documentation. |
| ground / earth | масса / заземление (по контексту документа) | The ground reference is marked on the diagram. |
| continuity | электрическая непрерывность цепи | The report records a continuity check result. |
| revision | редакция документа | Always identify the drawing revision in a report. |

**Terminology note:** *ground* and *earth* can vary by technical standard and context. Students must follow the terminology used in the specific approved document.

## 3. Reading a drawing: title, symbols and references
A technical drawing is more than a picture. Its title identifies the subject. A document number and revision identify the controlled issue. Labels connect the drawing to a parts list or another document. Symbols represent components and connections according to the drawing legend.

A connector is a physical joining component. A pin is an individual contact position. Bonding describes an electrical connection intended to maintain electrical continuity between conductive structures. A ground reference identifies a point or network according to the system documentation. These terms are related, but they are not interchangeable.

### Reading tasks
1. What information does a revision field provide?
2. Is a connector the same thing as a pin?
3. Why should a reader consult the drawing legend?
4. Why must *ground* be interpreted in context?

## 4. Language focus: location and reference
Useful phrases:
- “The connector is identified as…”
- “The wire is shown between… and…”
- “According to the drawing legend…”
- “The reference is located in the lower-right corner.”
- “The document revision is…”
- “The available information does not identify…”

Practise prepositions: **between, at, on, from, to, within**.

## 5. Listening: Drawing revision briefing

**Audio script for browser speech synthesis:**

“Please open the training wiring diagram, document number E-W-D zero one. Before reading the circuit, check the revision field and the drawing legend. The connector references are shown next to the symbols. Individual pin positions are identified in the connector view. The ground reference is marked according to the legend. Do not assume that two drawings are identical because their titles are similar. If the revision is different, record the document number and revision before you compare the information.”

**First listening — gist:** What should the student check before reading the circuit?

**Second listening — details:**
1. What is the example document number?
2. Where are connector references shown?
3. How are pin positions identified?
4. What must be recorded before comparing two drawings?

## 6. Practice
**A. Choose the correct term.**
1. A physical component used to join conductors: *connector / revision*.
2. An individual contact position: *pin / harness*.
3. A controlled issue identifier: *revision / load*.
4. A grouped set or route of wires: *harness / busbar*.

**B. Correct the statements.**
1. “A connector and a pin are exactly the same thing.”
2. “A drawing title alone always proves that two documents are identical.”
3. “Ground and bonding can be used as synonyms in every document.”

**C. Speaking in pairs.** Student A describes a fictional diagram reference. Student B asks for the document number, revision, connector reference and legend information. Use only the information on the teacher-provided training sheet.

## 7. Self-check — answers
Reading: 1-the controlled issue; 2-no, a pin is an individual contact position; 3-symbol meanings are defined there; 4-meaning depends on the applicable standard and document.  
Listening: 1-before reading the circuit; 2-E-W-D zero one; 3-next to the symbols; 4-the document number and revision.  
Practice A: 1-connector; 2-pin; 3-revision; 4-harness.  
Practice B: the statements are inaccurate; use the distinctions in the vocabulary and reading sections.

## 8. Independent work
Prepare a one-page annotated explanation of a teacher-provided, non-operational training wiring diagram. Label the title block, revision, legend, connector references and ground symbol. Add a 6–8 sentence explanation in English.

# Lesson 12. Electrical Measurements and Describing a Fault

**Duration:** 90 minutes

## Learning outcomes
Students can name common electrical measurement quantities and instruments, report values with units, distinguish an observation from a suspected cause, and write a concise fault description based on supplied evidence.

## 1. Lead-in
Discuss the difference between:
- a measurement;
- an observation;
- an interpretation;
- a confirmed cause.

A professional report must not present an assumption as a confirmed fault.

## 2. Key vocabulary

| English | Russian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| voltage | напряжение | The recorded voltage is shown in the report. |
| current | сила тока | The current value is stated with its unit. |
| resistance | сопротивление | The table includes a resistance value. |
| measurement | измерение | The measurement is linked to a test record. |
| multimeter | мультиметр | The training text names a multimeter. |
| reading | показание прибора | The reading is recorded with a time and unit. |
| unit | единица измерения | The unit must not be omitted. |
| nominal value | номинальное значение | The nominal value is taken from the specified document. |
| deviation | отклонение | The report describes a deviation from the stated range. |
| fault report | сообщение / отчёт о неисправности | The fault report separates evidence from assumptions. |

## 3. Language focus: reporting values and uncertainty
Use:
- “The recorded value is 24 volts.”
- “The measurement was taken at the point identified in the training case.”
- “The value is outside the range stated in the exercise.”
- “The observation may indicate a problem, but the cause has not been confirmed.”
- “Further inspection is required by authorised personnel.”

Use **was recorded / was observed / was reported** for passive reporting. Use **may, might, could** to express a possibility rather than a confirmed conclusion.

## 4. Reading: A classroom fault report

**Fictional training case — not an aircraft maintenance record**

A student receives a classroom data sheet for a simulated low-voltage indication. The sheet contains a reference value, a recorded value, a unit and a time stamp. The recorded value differs from the reference range printed on the exercise sheet. The student writes that a deviation was observed. The student does not identify a failed component because the supplied information does not prove the cause.

A good report includes the equipment or training-case identifier, document reference, recorded value and unit, comparison with the stated reference, time of observation and a clear statement of what is not yet known. It does not recommend an unauthorised action.

### Reading tasks
1. What is the difference between the reference value and the recorded value?
2. What evidence is available?
3. Has the cause been confirmed?
4. Name four items that should appear in the report.

## 5. Listening: Reporting a measurement

**Audio script for browser speech synthesis:**

“This is a classroom reporting exercise. The training sheet identifies case number E twenty-four. The reference range is printed in the exercise document. The recorded value is twenty-one point eight volts. The unit and time are included in the record. The value is below the reference range shown on this training sheet. This is an observation, not a confirmed diagnosis. The available information does not identify the cause. The case should be referred to the instructor for review.”

**First listening — gist:** Is the speaker giving a confirmed diagnosis or reporting an observation?

**Second listening — details:**
1. What is the training case number?
2. What value is recorded?
3. What is the unit?
4. How does the value compare with the exercise reference?
5. Has the cause been identified?

## 6. Practice
**A. Match the quantity and unit.**
1. voltage
2. current
3. resistance

a. ohm (Ω)  
b. ampere (A)  
c. volt (V)

**B. Rewrite cautiously.**
1. “The component is broken.” (Evidence: one recorded value is outside the exercise range.)
2. “The test proves the cause.” (Evidence: the case sheet contains only one measurement.)
3. “It is 21.8.” (Add the unit and identify it as a recorded value.)

**C. Complete the report.**
“Case ID: ______. The recorded ______ is ______ V. The value is ______ the reference range stated in the exercise. The cause has not been ______.”

**D. Writing.** Produce a 70–90-word fault observation report using the fictional case. Include the case ID, measurement, unit, comparison, time/reference and uncertainty statement.

## 7. Self-check — answers
Reading: 1-the reference is the comparison value; the recorded value is the measured value in the fictional case; 2-a recorded value, unit, time and reference range; 3-no; 4-case ID, document reference, value and unit, time, comparison and uncertainty statement.  
Listening: observation; E24; 21.8; volts; below the stated exercise range; no.  
Practice A: 1-c, 2-b, 3-a.  
Practice C: E24; value; 21.8; below; confirmed.

## 8. Independent work
Write a short report based on a teacher-provided fictional measurement table. Include at least two measurements, their units and a comparison with the exercise reference. Clearly separate facts from possible explanations.

- accurate interpretation of the supplied training information — 0–2;

- understandable spoken or written report — 0–1.
