# Lesson 10. Communication at Work: Requests and Clarification

**Suggested time:** 80–90 minutes  
**Main aim:** ask for information politely, clarify instructions and report that a message has or has not been understood.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, students can:
1. choose an appropriate polite request in a familiar study or workplace situation;
2. ask a speaker to repeat, explain or confirm information;
3. use indirect questions with `Could you tell me…?` and `Do you know…?`;
4. distinguish a request, an instruction and a confirmation;
5. take part in a short work-related dialogue and write a clear follow-up message.

## 1. Lead-in: when a message is unclear

Discuss:
- What can go wrong if a person guesses an instruction?
- Is it impolite to ask someone to repeat information?
- What details should you confirm before starting a task?
- How can you check that you and your colleague understand the same thing?

**Communication rule:** asking a clear question is safer and more professional than pretending to understand. Confirm important details such as an equipment ID, document revision, measurement unit, deadline or responsible person.

## 2. Vocabulary: useful communication phrases

| Phrase | Russian meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Could you repeat that, please? | Не могли бы вы повторить? | Could you repeat the last number, please? |
| Could you speak a little more slowly? | Не могли бы вы говорить немного медленнее? | Could you speak a little more slowly? I am taking notes. |
| What do you mean by…? | Что вы имеете в виду под…? | What do you mean by “final check”? |
| Could you clarify the instruction? | Не могли бы вы уточнить инструкцию? | Could you clarify the instruction before we begin? |
| Let me check that I understood. | Позвольте проверить, правильно ли я понял. | Let me check that I understood: revision C, not B. |
| Do you mean…? | Вы имеете в виду…? | Do you mean the left-hand connector? |
| Could you confirm…? | Не могли бы вы подтвердить…? | Could you confirm the delivery time? |
| According to the document… | Согласно документу… | According to the document, the check is due today. |
| I will get back to you. | Я вернусь к вам с ответом. | I will check the schedule and get back to you. |
| I am not sure I follow. | Не уверен, что понял. | I am not sure I follow. Could you give an example? |

### Communication functions

- **Request:** Could you send me the updated file?
- **Clarification:** Which version do you mean?
- **Confirmation:** So the meeting starts at 10:30, correct?
- **Correction:** Sorry, I meant Thursday, not Tuesday.
- **Acknowledgement:** Understood. I will update the record.

### Practice 1. Match the situation and phrase

1. You did not hear an equipment number.
2. You want to check whether you understood the date correctly.
3. A technical term is unfamiliar.
4. You need the latest document.
5. You cannot answer immediately but will check.

a) “I will check and get back to you.”  
b) “Could you send me the latest revision?”  
c) “Could you repeat the number, please?”  
d) “What do you mean by this term?”  
e) “So the deadline is Friday, correct?”

## 3. Grammar focus: polite requests and indirect questions

### Direct and polite questions

| Direct | More polite |
|---|---|
| Send me the file. | Could you send me the file, please? |
| Repeat the number. | Could you repeat the number, please? |
| Where is the report? | Could you tell me where the report is? |
| When does the test start? | Do you know when the test starts? |

Use **Could you…?** to make a request. Use **Could you tell me…? / Do you know…?** to ask for information politely.

### Word order in indirect questions

A direct question often uses an auxiliary before the subject:
- Where **is** the document?
- When **does** the inspection start?

An indirect question uses statement word order:
- Could you tell me where the document **is**?
- Do you know when the inspection **starts**?

Do not use question word order after the introductory phrase:
- Incorrect: *Could you tell me where is the document?*
- Correct: *Could you tell me where the document is?*

For yes/no questions, use **if** or **whether**:
- Is the room available? → Could you tell me **if the room is available**?
- Has the file been updated? → Do you know **whether the file has been updated**?

### Practice 2. Rewrite politely

1. “Give me the drawing.”
2. “Where does the group meet?”
3. “Is the equipment ready?”
4. “Repeat the last instruction.”
5. “When will the results be available?”

**Recording script — reveal after the tasks**

**Supervisor:** Have you got the job sheet for the training task?  
**Student:** Yes, but I can see two files in the shared folder. Could you confirm which revision we should use?  
**Supervisor:** Use revision B. The file name includes the date of the latest update.  
**Student:** Let me check that I understood: revision B, and the task is due on Wednesday afternoon. Is that correct?  
**Supervisor:** That's correct. Please attach the measurement table when you submit the worksheet.  
**Student:** Understood. One more question: should the table include all three sample parts?

**Student:** Thank you. I'll use revision B and include the three measurements.

**Language focus**
- `Could you confirm…?` — polite request for confirmation.
- `Let me check that I understood…` — signals a summary.
- `Is that correct?` — invites confirmation.
- `If any value is unclear, do not guess.` — conditional instruction.

- a task deadline;
- one required attachment;
- one point that needs clarification.

# Lesson 11. Travel, Transport and Travel Problems

**Suggested time:** 80–90 minutes  
**Main aim:** understand travel information, ask for directions and deal with common transport problems during a study trip or work placement.

## Learning outcomes

Students can:
1. name common forms of transport and travel documents;
2. ask for and understand directions;
3. use comparative language to discuss travel options;
4. understand an announcement and a short conversation about a delayed journey;
5. write a short message explaining a travel delay and giving an updated arrival time.

## 1. Lead-in: travelling for study and work

Discuss:
- How do students usually travel to college?
- What transport might a specialist use to attend training in another city?
- What information should you check before leaving?
- What would you do if your train or bus were delayed?

Travel planning involves checking the route, departure time, platform or stop, ticket conditions, baggage rules and a backup option. For a work placement, it is important to inform the responsible person if a delay affects arrival.

## 2. Vocabulary: transport and travel

| arrival | прибытие | The arrival time has changed. |
| platform | платформа, путь | The train leaves from platform 4. |
| timetable | расписание | Check the timetable before you travel. |
| transfer | пересадка | We have one transfer in the city centre. |
| delay | задержка | There is a 20-minute delay. |
| cancellation | отмена | The cancellation affects the evening service. |
| connection | стыковка / пересадка | I may miss my connection. |
| route | маршрут | This route is shorter but more expensive. |

### Useful travel questions

- Which platform does the train leave from?
- How long does the journey take?
- Do I need to change trains?
- Is this the fastest route?
- Where can I find the departure board?
- Could you tell me how to get to the training centre?

## 3. Grammar focus: comparatives and superlatives for travel choices

Use comparatives to compare two options:
- The train is **faster than** the bus.
- This route is **more convenient than** the other one.
- The second connection is **less expensive than** the first.

Use superlatives to compare three or more:
- This is **the fastest** route.
- It is **the most reliable** option today.
- That is **the least expensive** ticket.

Irregular forms:
- good → better → the best;
- bad → worse → the worst;
- far → farther/further → the farthest/furthest.

**As…as** shows equality:
- The morning service is as convenient as the afternoon service.

**Not as…as** shows a difference:
- The bus is not as fast as the train.

### Practice 2. Choose the correct form

5. Which route is the (shorter / shortest) of the three?

**Recording script — reveal after the tasks**

**Passenger:** Excuse me. I have a connecting train at 09:45. Do you know if I will have enough time to change platforms?

- `estimated arrival time` — a predicted, not guaranteed, time;
- `I cannot guarantee that` — careful language when information is uncertain;

# Lesson 12. Reading Strategies: Main Idea, Detail and Context

**Suggested time:** 90 minutes  
**Main aim:** read unfamiliar everyday, educational and introductory technical texts more efficiently by identifying purpose, main idea, details and meaning from context.

## Learning outcomes

Students can:
1. identify the purpose and main idea of a short text;
2. scan for names, dates, numbers, conditions and instructions;
3. infer the meaning of an unfamiliar word from context;
4. distinguish a fact from an opinion and a stated fact from an assumption;
5. read a short technical-style notice and extract information without translating every word.

## 1. Lead-in: do you need to translate every word?

Discuss:
- What do you do when you meet an unfamiliar word?
- Can you understand a text without knowing every word?
- When is it better to read quickly?
- When do you need to read carefully?

**Four practical strategies**
1. **Skimming** — read quickly to understand the topic and general idea.
2. **Scanning** — search for a particular detail, such as a date, number or name.
3. **Careful reading** — examine instructions, conditions and relationships.
4. **Guessing from context** — use nearby words, examples, contrast and the situation to infer meaning.

Do not translate every word automatically. First decide what information the task requires.

## 2. Vocabulary: text structure and meaning

| Term | Russian | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| heading | заголовок | the topic or purpose |
| paragraph | абзац | one main point or stage |
| main idea | основная мысль | what the text is mainly about |
| detail | подробность | a specific fact or example |
| purpose | цель | why the text was written |
| instruction | инструкция | what the reader must do |
| condition | условие | when something is allowed or required |
| evidence | доказательство, подтверждение | information supporting a statement |
| reference | ссылка / обозначение | where information comes from or what it identifies |
| context | контекст | surrounding information that helps explain meaning |
| although | хотя | introduces contrast |
| therefore | поэтому | introduces a result |
| unless | если не | introduces an exception or condition |

### Signal words

- **Addition:** and, also, furthermore.
- **Contrast:** but, however, although.
- **Reason:** because, since.
- **Result:** so, therefore, as a result.
- **Sequence:** first, next, then, finally.
- **Condition:** if, unless, provided that.

### Practice 1. Identify the reading strategy

Choose **skimming**, **scanning**, **careful reading** or **guessing from context**.

1. You need to find the departure time in a long timetable.
2. You want to understand what a notice is generally about.
3. You need to understand a safety instruction before following it.
4. You do not know the word “portable”, but the sentence says the device is small and easy to carry.
5. You need to find the serial number in a maintenance record.

## 3. Reading skill: main idea and supporting details

A main idea is broader than one detail.

Example:
- **Main idea:** The college is introducing a new digital system for recording practical work.
- **Detail:** Students must upload their worksheets by Friday.

A useful method:
1. Read the title.
2. Read the first sentence of each paragraph.
3. Notice repeated words and ideas.
4. Ask: “What does the whole text mainly explain?”
5. Check that your answer is not just one small detail.

## 4. Reading: an introductory technical notice

Read the notice once quickly. Then read it again to answer the questions.

### Practical class notice — measuring a sample component

**Purpose**  
The group will practise recording dimensions of a sample component and comparing the results with the values shown on the classroom drawing.

- Give the worksheet to the instructor.

## 5. Listening: finding information in a short announcement

**Before you listen**

**First listening — gist**

A) A change to the worksheet submission procedure.  
B) A college sports event.  
C) A new cafeteria menu.

**Second listening — details**

1. What is the new deadline?
   A) Tuesday at 12:00  B) Wednesday at 16:00  C) Friday at 09:00
2. Which field was missing from some submissions?
   A) task number  B) home address  C) favourite subject

   A) send a message to the instructor before the deadline

   B) for one minute  
   C) they should throw them away immediately

**Recording script — reveal after the tasks**

**Instructor:** Good afternoon. I have an update about the practical worksheet. The submission deadline is Wednesday at 16:00, not Tuesday at noon as stated in the first notice.

Please check that your file includes your group, the date and the task number. Several submissions were missing the task number, so I could not identify the correct activity.

If you have a problem uploading the file, send me a message before the deadline and explain what happened. Do not send an empty file. Keep your original notes until I confirm that your digital submission is complete.

Thank you.

**Language focus**
- `not Tuesday at noon as stated in the first notice` — correction of earlier information;
- `includes your group, the date and the task number` — a list of required details;
- `If you have a problem…, send me…` — a conditional instruction;
- `until I confirm` — identifies when the requirement ends.

**After listening**
In pairs, one student gives a short announcement about a changed classroom deadline. The other takes notes under four headings: **what changed / new time / required information / what to do if there is a problem**. Compare notes and ask clarification questions.

### D. Integrated task

Read this message:

“Hello. The training visit will start 30 minutes later because the morning bus is delayed. Please meet outside the main entrance at 10:00 instead of 09:30. Bring your college ID and notebook. If you cannot arrive by 10:00, contact the group leader.”

Answer:
1. What has changed?
2. What is the new meeting time?
3. Name two things students should bring.
4. What should a student do if they cannot arrive on time?
5. Which words explain the reason for the change?

### Review key

A: 1 repeat; 2 understood; 3 confirm; 4 back.
