### Read the text and answer Questions 91–95.

*Many students use online dictionaries when they study a foreign language. These tools can provide pronunciation, example sentences and information about word forms. However, a dictionary entry does not always show which expression is most natural in a particular situation. For this reason, learners should read the examples and notice the words that commonly appear together. It is also useful to compare a new word in two or three reliable sources. Teachers advise students not to translate every sentence word for word: understanding the main idea first often makes reading faster and more effective.*

## Teacher key and rationale

| No. | Key | Rationale |
|---:|:---:|---|
| 81 | A | An indirect question uses statement word order: “where the bus stop is”. |
| 82 | C | “Would rather” is followed by the bare infinitive. |
| 83 | B | A completed event in a finished time period (“in one evening”) takes past simple. |
| 84 | D | “Needn't” is followed by the bare infinitive. |
| 85 | A | “Whose” indicates possession: the student's project. |
| 86 | B | “Because” introduces the reason for being late. |
| 87 | C | “Since” introduces the starting point of an action continuing to the present. |
| 88 | D | “Suggest” can be followed by a gerund. |
| 89 | B | “Need” can be followed by a gerund with passive meaning: the instructions need rewriting. |
| 90 | A | “Selfish” is the opposite of “generous”. |
| 91 | B | The first two sentences list pronunciation and example sentences among dictionary features. |
| 92 | C | Examples show how words and expressions are used in context. |
| 93 | A | The text recommends comparing a new word in two or three reliable sources. |
| 94 | D | The final sentence says understanding the main idea first often makes reading faster. |
| 95 | B | The passage describes benefits and limitations of online dictionaries and gives advice on using context. |
| 96 | C | Third conditional: “If + past perfect, would have + past participle”. |
| 97 | B | Future passive is “will be + past participle”. |
| 98 | A | This phrase acknowledges the other view before expressing disagreement politely. |
| 99 | C | “Reliable” means dependable or trustworthy. |
| 100 | B | “In order to” introduces the purpose of checking the details. |
