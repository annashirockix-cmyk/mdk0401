## Lesson 04. Future Forms: Plans, Arrangements and Predictions

**Level:** A2 (with B1 extension) · **Suggested time:** 80 minutes

### Learning outcomes
Students will distinguish a decision made now, a prior intention, an arrangement and a prediction; form sentences with *will*, *be going to* and Present Continuous for future meaning; ask follow-up questions; understand a short conversation about study and career plans.

### 1. Lead-in
Discuss: What are you going to do after college? Are you doing anything this weekend? What do you think engineering will look like in ten years? Separate a personal plan from a prediction.

### 2. Three common future forms

| Form | Main use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **will + infinitive** | decision made at the moment; offer; prediction or opinion | The phone is ringing. I'll answer it. / I think aircraft will become more energy-efficient. |
| **be going to + infinitive** | prior intention; prediction based on present evidence | We are going to revise for the exam. / Look at those clouds. It is going to rain. |
| **Present Continuous** | fixed personal arrangement, often with time/place | I'm meeting my tutor at 3 p.m. |

Forms:
- Affirmative: *I will study; she is going to apply; we are meeting tomorrow.*
- Negative: *I won't forget; he isn't going to leave; they aren't travelling on Friday.*
- Questions: *Will you help? Are you going to apply? Are you meeting the group?*

Contractions: *I'll, won't, we're, she's*. In formal writing, avoid overusing contractions.

### 3. Important contrasts
- *I'll help you with that box.* — spontaneous offer.
- *I'm going to learn more technical English.* — intention already formed.
- *I'm seeing the careers adviser on Thursday.* — arranged appointment.
- *I think the project will be useful.* — opinion/prediction.

Do not use *will* automatically after every future time expression. In a time clause referring to the future, English normally uses Present Simple: *When the lesson finishes, we'll go to the library* (not *when the lesson will finish*).

### 4. Vocabulary
*plan* — планировать; *intend* — намереваться; *arrange* — договариваться; *appointment* — встреча по записи; *apply for* — подавать заявление на; *qualification* — квалификация; *prediction* — прогноз; *evidence* — свидетельство; *schedule* — расписание; *career adviser* — консультант по карьере.

### 5. Reading: A Plan for the Next Year
*Daria is in her second year at college. She is going to improve her English because she wants to read technical manuals in the original. She has already chosen an online course and is starting it next Monday. On Friday, she is meeting a teacher to discuss her study plan. Daria thinks digital tools will change many engineering jobs, but she knows that practical skills will remain important. “I won't stop practising,” she says. “Technology will develop, so we will have to keep learning.”*

**Gist:** What is Daria's main goal?

**Detail:** 1. Why is she improving English? 2. What has she chosen? 3. When does the course start? 4. Who is she meeting on Friday? 5. What does she think will remain important?

### 6. Listening — “Plans after College”
**Before listening:** *apprenticeship* — производственное обучение; *application* — заявление; *interview* — собеседование; *schedule* — расписание.

**Original script for teacher recording (about 60–70 seconds):**
> Tutor: Have you made any plans for next year, Pavel?  
> Pavel: Yes. I'm going to finish my college course and apply for an apprenticeship at an aircraft components plant.  
> Tutor: Have you arranged an interview?  
> Pavel: Not yet. I'm meeting the careers adviser on Wednesday. She'll help me prepare my application.  
> Tutor: Good. What will you do if you don't get the first place?  
> Pavel: I'll apply to two other companies. I don't want to stop learning.  
> Tutor: That's a sensible plan. Are you taking an English course this summer?  
> Pavel: Yes, I'm starting one in July. I think it will help me understand technical documents.

**First listening — gist:** What is Pavel planning?

**Second listening — details:** 1. What kind of opportunity will he apply for? 2. When is he meeting the adviser? 3. How many other companies will he contact if necessary? 4. When does the English course start? 5. Why does he want to study English?

**Language focus:** Identify one example of *going to*, one Present Continuous future arrangement and two uses of *will*.

**After listening:** In pairs, conduct a tutor–student interview about plans after college. Ask at least four questions.

*Teacher note: script only; it must be recorded before it can be used as playable audio. Show the transcript after the listening activity.*

### 7. Practice
**A. Choose the best form.**
1. The doorbell is ringing. I (will / am going to) open the door.
2. We have booked the tickets. We (are flying / will fly) on 12 June.
3. Look at the dark sky. It (is going to / will) snow.
4. I think robots (are replacing / will replace) some repetitive tasks.
5. She has decided: she (is going to / will) apply for the course.

**B. Complete with the correct form of the verb.**
1. I ______ (meet) my group at 10 tomorrow; it is arranged.
2. They ______ (not / forget) the deadline, I promise.
3. What ______ you ______ (do) after the exam?
4. When the workshop ______ (finish), we will discuss the results.

**C. Correct the mistakes.**
1. When I will graduate, I will look for a job.
2. She going to study electronics.
3. We will meeting the teacher tomorrow.

### 8. Self-check
1. Which form often expresses a prior intention?
2. Which form is natural for a fixed appointment?
3. Complete: *I think the weather ___ improve.*
4. Correct: *When the train will arrive, I'll call you.*
5. Write three sentences: one spontaneous decision, one intention and one arrangement.

**Teacher key**
- Reading: improving English to read technical manuals; online course; next Monday; a teacher; practical skills.
- Listening: apprenticeship at an aircraft components plant; Wednesday; two; July; to understand technical documents.
- A: 1 will; 2 are flying; 3 is going to; 4 will replace; 5 is going to.
- B: 1 am meeting; 2 won't forget; 3 are / going to do (also *will you do* where context is a neutral question); 4 finishes.
- C: 1 When I graduate, I will look for a job. 2 She is going to study electronics. 3 We are meeting the teacher tomorrow.
- Self-check: 1 be going to; 2 Present Continuous; 3 will; 4 When the train arrives, I'll call you. Item 5: assess correct form and meaning.

### 9. Independent work
Write 100–120 words titled **My Plans for the Next Two Years**. Include a study goal, a career intention, one fixed arrangement, one prediction and one sentence with *when* or *if* referring to the future.

## Lesson 05. Comparatives, Superlatives and Describing Change

**Level:** A2–B1 · **Suggested time:** 80 minutes

### Learning outcomes
Students can compare people, places, tools and processes; form comparative and superlative adjectives; use *as…as, less…than, much/a little*; describe simple changes in a table or short technical comparison.

### 1. Lead-in
Compare two ways to travel to college, two study methods or two devices. Avoid unsupported claims: say what feature you are comparing (price, weight, speed, reliability, convenience).

### 2. Comparative forms

| Adjective | Comparative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| small | smaller | This component is smaller than that one. |
| fast | faster | The new process is faster. |
| easy | easier | This exercise is easier than the previous one. |
| large | larger | Model B is larger than Model A. |
| useful | more useful | This diagram is more useful for beginners. |
| reliable | more reliable | The second method is more reliable. |

Spelling:
- short adjective + **-er**: *quick → quicker*;
- final **-e** + **-r**: *safe → safer*;
- consonant + **y** → **-ier**: *heavy → heavier*;
- some short consonant-vowel-consonant adjectives double the final consonant: *big → bigger*;
- longer adjectives usually use **more**: *more accurate*.

Irregular forms: *good → better; bad → worse; far → farther/further*.

Use **than** after a comparative: *A is lighter than B.*

### 3. Superlatives and equality
- Short adjectives: *the + adjective-est*: *the fastest, the safest*.
- Longer adjectives: *the most + adjective*: *the most reliable*.
- Irregular: *the best, the worst, the farthest/furthest*.
- Equality: *as accurate as*.
- Inequality: *not as expensive as*.
- Lower degree: *less noisy than*.

Use **much / far** for a strong difference (*much faster*) and **a little / slightly** for a small difference (*slightly heavier*).

### 4. Describing change
Useful verbs and phrases:
- *increase / rise* — увеличиваться / расти;
- *decrease / fall* — уменьшаться / снижаться;
- *remain stable* — оставаться стабильным;
- *become more efficient* — становиться эффективнее;
- *from…to…* — с… до…;
- *by 10 per cent* — на 10 процентов.

Example: *The processing time fell from 50 minutes to 40 minutes. The new method is 10 minutes faster.*

### 5. Reading: Choosing a Measurement Tool
*A workshop needs a tool for checking the diameter of small components. A ruler is cheaper and easier to use, but it is less precise for very small measurements. A vernier caliper is more accurate for many workshop tasks. A micrometer is usually the most suitable of these three tools when a very small dimension must be measured precisely. However, the best choice depends on the required accuracy, the component and the measurement procedure. A more expensive instrument is not automatically the right instrument for every task.*

**Gist:** What is the text comparing?

**Detail:** 1. Which tool is cheapest? 2. Which is described as the most suitable for very small dimensions? 3. Why is a ruler less suitable for some tasks? 4. Does the text say that the most expensive tool is always best? 5. Name two factors that influence the choice.

### 6. Listening — “Comparing Two Methods”
**Before listening:** *cycle time* — продолжительность цикла; *sample* — образец; *accurate* — точный; *result* — результат.

**Original script for teacher recording (about 60–70 seconds):**
> Engineer: We tested two methods for checking the same sample. Method A took twelve minutes. Method B took nine minutes.  
> Student: So Method B was faster. Was it also accurate?  
> Engineer: Yes. Both methods gave results within the required range, but Method B was easier to repeat.  
> Student: Was it more expensive?  
> Engineer: The equipment for Method B costs more, so we must consider the budget.  
> Student: Which method is the best?  
> Engineer: We cannot decide from speed alone. We need to compare accuracy, repeatability, cost and the requirements of the task.

**First listening:** What is being compared?

**Second listening:** 1. How long did Method A take? 2. How long did Method B take? 3. Were both results within the required range? 4. Which method was easier to repeat? 5. What factors must be considered before choosing?

**Language focus:** Find two comparative adjectives and one superlative. Explain why the engineer does not choose based on speed alone.

**After listening:** Compare two study methods or two devices using at least four comparative forms.

*Teacher note: script for recording, not a supplied audio file.*

### 7. Practice
**A. Write the comparative and superlative.**
1. safe 2. heavy 3. reliable 4. good 5. small 6. expensive.

**B. Complete.**
1. A train is usually ______ (fast) than a bus over a long distance.
2. This is the ______ (accurate) result in the table.
3. My new notebook is not as ______ (heavy) as the old one.
4. Method A is ______ (slightly / cheap) than Method B.
5. The temperature became ______ (low) after the system was switched off.

**C. Use the data.**

| Method | Time | Cost | Repeatability |
|---|---:|---|---|
| A | 12 min | low | good |
| B | 9 min | high | very good |
| C | 15 min | low | acceptable |

Write four sentences comparing the methods. Do not claim that one is universally best; explain the criterion.

**D. Correct the mistakes.**
1. This tool is more cheaper.
2. It is the most easiest task.
3. The new part is as lighter as the old one.

### 8. Self-check
1. Comparative of *good*?
2. Superlative of *reliable*?
3. Complete: *This sample is ___ accurate than the first one.*
4. Correct: *The second method is more faster.*
5. Write one sentence with *as…as* and one with *less…than*.

**Teacher key**
- Reading: ruler; micrometer; it is less precise for very small measurements; no; required accuracy, component, procedure.
- Listening: two checking methods; 12 minutes; 9 minutes; yes; Method B; accuracy, repeatability, cost and task requirements.
- A: safer / safest; heavier / heaviest; more reliable / most reliable; better / best; smaller / smallest; more expensive / most expensive.
- B: 1 faster; 2 most accurate; 3 heavy; 4 slightly cheaper; 5 lower.
- D: 1 This tool is cheaper. 2 It is the easiest task. 3 The new part is as light as the old one.
- Self-check: 1 better; 2 the most reliable; 3 more; 4 The second method is faster. 5 accept correct examples.
- For table task, accept comparisons supported by data: B is the fastest and has very good repeatability, while A and C have lower cost; C takes the longest time.

### 9. Independent work
Choose two devices, transport options or study methods. Create a comparison table with at least three criteria. Write 100–120 words using at least six comparative/superlative structures and one phrase describing change.

## Lesson 06. Modal Verbs: Advice, Obligation, Permission and Ability

**Level:** A2–B1 · **Suggested time:** 80 minutes

### Learning outcomes
Students can express ability, permission, advice, obligation and prohibition; distinguish *mustn't* from *don't have to*; make polite requests; understand a short safety-oriented briefing without treating classroom language as a substitute for official procedures.

### 1. Lead-in
Which phrases express ability, advice, obligation or prohibition?
- You can use this dictionary.
- You should check the instructions.
- You must wear the required protective equipment.
- You mustn't touch the machine while it is operating.

### 2. Core modal verbs

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| can / could | ability; informal or polite request | She can read the diagram. Could you repeat that? |
| may | formal permission / possibility | May I come in? |
| must | strong obligation; speaker's authority or conclusion | You must follow the approved procedure. |
| have to | necessity imposed by rules or circumstances | We have to submit the report today. |
| should | advice or recommendation | You should check the units. |
| mustn't | prohibition | You mustn't remove the guard. |
| don't have to | no necessity; optional | You don't have to print the document. |
| might / may | possibility | The reading might be incorrect. |

After a central modal (*can, could, may, must, should, might*) use the infinitive without **to**: *must check*, not *must to check*. With *have to*, use **to**: *have to check*.

### 3. Important contrasts
- **mustn't** = it is not allowed; do not do it.
- **don't have to** = it is not necessary, but it is allowed.
- **should** = advice, not usually a strict rule.
- **must / have to** = obligation; the choice depends on context and variety of English.
- **could you…? / would you…?** = polite request.
- **May I…?** = formal request for permission.

Examples:
- You **mustn't** share your password. (prohibited)
- You **don't have to** bring a printed copy. (optional)
- You **should** save a backup copy. (advice)

### 4. Vocabulary
*permission* — разрешение; *obligation* — обязанность; *prohibition* — запрет; *protective equipment* — средства защиты; *approved procedure* — утверждённая процедура; *supervisor* — руководитель; *hazard* — опасность; *report a fault* — сообщить о неисправности; *disconnect* — отключить; *authorised* — уполномоченный, имеющий допуск.

### 5. Reading: Rules in a Training Workshop
*Students must follow the teacher's instructions in the training workshop. They have to wear the protective equipment required for the particular activity. They mustn't operate equipment without permission or remove a safety guard. If a tool looks damaged, students should stop the task and report the problem to the teacher. They don't have to memorise every technical specification, but they must know where the approved instructions are kept. Only authorised staff may change machine settings.*

**Gist:** What is the text mainly about?

**Detail:** 1. What must students follow? 2. When may they operate equipment? 3. What should they do if a tool looks damaged? 4. Do they have to memorise every specification? 5. Who may change machine settings?

### 6. Listening — “A Workshop Briefing”
**Before listening:** *authorised* — имеющий разрешение; *guard* — защитное ограждение; *report* — сообщать; *supervisor* — руководитель.

**Original script for teacher recording (about 60–70 seconds):**
> Supervisor: Before we start, remember the workshop rules. You must wear the protective equipment specified for today's task. You mustn't start the machine until I give permission.  
> Student: Do we have to check the work area first?  
> Supervisor: Yes. You should check that the area is clear and report any visible damage. Only authorised staff may change the machine settings.  
> Student: Could I ask you to explain the emergency stop procedure again?  
> Supervisor: Of course. You must know the approved procedure before working. If anything is unclear, ask me. Do not guess.

**First listening:** What is the supervisor doing?

**Second listening:** 1. What must the student wear? 2. When can the machine be started? 3. What should be checked? 4. Who may change settings? 5. What should the student do if something is unclear?

**Language focus:** Find examples of obligation, prohibition, advice, permission and a polite request.

**After listening:** In pairs, write and present five general classroom/workshop rules. Do not invent machine-specific operating instructions.

*Teacher note: original recording script only. It does not replace local safety instructions or equipment manuals.*

### 7. Practice
**A. Choose the correct phrase.**
1. You (mustn't / don't have to) share your account password.
2. You (mustn't / don't have to) print the file; a digital copy is accepted.
3. Students (should / might) ask when an instruction is unclear.
4. (May / Must) I borrow your dictionary?
5. The sensor reading (might / mustn't) be incorrect.

**B. Rewrite using the modal in brackets.**
1. It is necessary to submit the form today. (*have to*)
2. It is a good idea to save your work. (*should*)
3. It is forbidden to enter this room without permission. (*mustn't*)
4. Is it possible for you to repeat the question? (*could*)

**C. Correct the mistakes.**
1. You must to wear safety glasses.
2. He don't have to come today.
3. You mustn't to touch the control.
4. May you help me, please? (rewrite as a polite request)

### 8. Self-check
1. What is the difference between *mustn't* and *don't have to*?
2. Complete: *You ___ report a damaged tool.* (advice)
3. Complete: *___ I ask a question?* (formal permission)
4. Correct: *She can to use the software.*
5. Write one prohibition, one piece of advice and one polite request.

**Teacher key**
- Reading: workshop rules; follow teacher's instructions; only after permission; stop the task and report it; no; authorised staff.
- Listening: briefing; specified protective equipment; after permission; work area; authorised staff; ask the supervisor.
- A: 1 mustn't; 2 don't have to; 3 should; 4 May; 5 might.
- B: 1 You have to submit the form today. 2 You should save your work. 3 You mustn't enter this room without permission. 4 Could you repeat the question?
- C: 1 You must wear safety glasses. 2 He doesn't have to come today. 3 You mustn't touch the control. 4 Could you help me, please?
- Self-check: *mustn't* is prohibition; *don't have to* means no necessity; 2 should; 3 May; 4 She can use the software. Item 5: accept accurate examples.

### 9. Independent work
Create a one-page **Student Guide to Responsible Workshop Communication**. Include 8–10 rules or recommendations, using at least four different modal structures. Add a short paragraph explaining the difference between a rule, a prohibition and advice.

## Semester review: Future, comparison and modal meaning

1. Write a 90–110-word plan for a future study or career goal.
2. Compare two methods or devices using at least four comparative/superlative forms.
3. Write six workshop or classroom statements: two obligations, two prohibitions, one recommendation and one polite request.
4. Listen to the three lesson scripts and identify the main idea and at least three details in each.

**Review criteria:** form and meaning of future structures; accurate comparative forms; distinction between prohibition and lack of necessity; clarity and politeness; listening for gist and detail.
