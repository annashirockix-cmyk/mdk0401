## Lesson 10. Past Simple: Regular and Irregular Verbs

**Level:** A1–A2  
**Suggested lesson time:** 80 minutes  
**Focus:** describing completed events, asking about the past, understanding a short account.

### Learning outcomes

By the end of the lesson, students can:
- identify when a speaker refers to a finished past event;
- form affirmative Past Simple sentences with regular and common irregular verbs;
- make questions and negatives with *did / didn't*;
- find the time, place and main events in a short past-tense text;
- give a short account of yesterday or a recent college day.

### 1. Lead-in: past or present?

Compare:
- I study English every week. — a regular activity.
- I studied English yesterday. — a completed event in the past.
- We visit the workshop on Mondays. — a routine.
- We visited the workshop last Monday. — one finished visit.

Common time expressions: **yesterday, last night, last week, two days ago, in 2024, on Monday**. They often signal a finished past time.

### 2. Grammar: affirmative forms

For most regular verbs, add **-ed**:
- work → worked
- visit → visited
- clean → cleaned

Spelling:
- live → lived (add -d after final *e*);
- study → studied (consonant + *y* changes to *-ied*);
- stop → stopped (in a short stressed consonant-vowel-consonant pattern, double the final consonant).

Some frequent verbs are irregular and must be learned:
| Infinitive | Past Simple | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| be | was / were | быть |
| go | went | идти, ехать |
| have | had | иметь |
| do | did | делать |
| see | saw | видеть |
| make | made | делать, создавать |
| get | got | получать |
| take | took | брать |
| come | came | приходить |
| write | wrote | писать |
| read | read /red/ | читать |
| meet | met | встречать(ся) |

Examples:
- We **visited** the college library yesterday.
- Our group **went** to the computer room.
- I **wrote** three sentences in my notebook.

### 3. Questions and negatives

Use **did + base form** in questions and **didn't + base form** in negatives. The main verb does not take the past ending after *did*.

| Form | Example |
|---|---|
| Statement | She checked the timetable. |
| Negative | She didn't check the timetable. |
| Question | Did she check the timetable? |
| Short answer | Yes, she did. / No, she didn't. |

Incorrect: *Did you went?*  
Correct: **Did you go?**

The verb **be** is different:
- I / he / she **was**; you / we / they **were**.
- **Was** the classroom open? **Were** the students ready?
- The workshop **wasn't** open.

### 4. Reading: A Busy College Day

*On Tuesday, our group had a busy day. We arrived at college at eight thirty. First, we attended an English lesson and revised new words. After the break, we went to the library. I borrowed a technical dictionary, but my friend Marina chose a book about aircraft. In the afternoon, we completed a computer task and sent our work to the teacher. We didn't stay late because our last class finished at three.*

**Read for gist:** What kind of day did the students have?

**Read for detail:**
1. What time did the group arrive?
2. Where did they go after the break?
3. What did the narrator borrow?
4. What did Marina choose?
5. Why didn't they stay late?

### 5. Listening task — “What did you do yesterday?”

**Before you listen:** check these words: *repair* — ремонтировать; *borrow* — брать на время; *finish* — заканчивать; *return* — возвращать.

**Listening script for teacher recording (about 45–55 seconds):**

> Alex: Hi, Nina. How was your day yesterday?  
> Nina: It was busy. I got up early and came to college by bus.  
> Alex: Did you have your English lesson first?  
> Nina: No, we had mathematics first. Then our group visited the library. I borrowed a book and returned another one.  
> Alex: Did you go to the workshop?  
> Nina: Yes. We watched a demonstration, but we didn't use the machines. The lesson finished at half past two.  
> Alex: What did you do in the evening?  
> Nina: I prepared my homework and called my cousin.

**First listening — general meaning:** Was Nina's day quiet or busy?

**Second listening — details:** Choose or write the answer.
1. How did Nina travel to college? (by bus / on foot / by train)
2. Which lesson did the group have first? (English / mathematics / physics)
3. What did Nina borrow? (a dictionary / a book / a laptop)
4. Did the students use the machines? (yes / no)
5. What time did the lesson finish? (2:00 / 2:30 / 3:30)

**Language focus:** Listen again and identify three past-tense verbs. Which are irregular?

**After listening:** In pairs, ask and answer: *What did you do yesterday? Where did you go? What did you study?*

*Teacher note: this is an original recording script, not an existing audio file. Record it at a natural, clear pace; provide the transcript after the listening attempt.*

### 6. Practice

**A. Put the verbs into Past Simple.**
1. We (visit) the library yesterday.
2. I (write) a short message.
3. They (go) to college by bus.
4. Marina (study) English in the evening.
5. The lesson (finish) at three.

**B. Make the sentences negative.**
1. I watched a film yesterday.
2. We went to the workshop.
3. He borrowed my dictionary.

**C. Make questions.**
1. You finished your homework.
2. They visited the library.
3. She took the bus.

**D. Correct the mistakes.**
1. Did you saw the teacher?
2. He didn't went to college.
3. We studyed English yesterday.

### 7. Self-check

1. Choose: *Yesterday I (go / went) to college.*
2. Complete: *Did they ___ (visit) the library?*
3. Make negative: *She wrote an email.*
4. Give the past form: *study, have, take.*
5. Write two sentences about something you did last week.

**Answer key for teacher**
- Practice A: 1 visited; 2 wrote; 3 went; 4 studied; 5 finished.
- Practice B: 1 I didn't watch a film yesterday. 2 We didn't go to the workshop. 3 He didn't borrow my dictionary.
- Practice C: 1 Did you finish your homework? 2 Did they visit the library? 3 Did she take the bus?
- Practice D: 1 Did you see the teacher? 2 He didn't go to college. 3 We studied English yesterday.
- Self-check: 1 went; 2 visit; 3 She didn't write an email; 4 studied, had, took. Item 5: accept grammatically correct, meaningful answers.

### 8. Independent work

Write 8–10 sentences under the title **“My Last College Day”**. Include at least five Past Simple verbs, two time expressions, one negative sentence and one question you would ask a classmate. Underline irregular verbs.

## Lesson 11. Countable and Uncountable Nouns; Some, Any, Much, Many

**Level:** A1–A2  
**Suggested lesson time:** 80 minutes  
**Focus:** describing quantities, asking about supplies and shopping politely.

### Learning outcomes

Students can:
- distinguish countable and uncountable nouns in common contexts;
- use singular and plural forms with *a / an* and numbers;
- choose *some / any, much / many, a lot of* in basic sentences;
- ask about quantities and understand a short shopping conversation.

### 1. Lead-in

Look at the classroom or workshop. You can count **chairs, books, pens, computers**. You usually do not count **water, milk, rice, information, equipment** as separate units.

### 2. Countable nouns

Countable nouns have singular and plural forms:
- one notebook — two notebooks;
- a tool — three tools;
- an apple — five apples.

Use **a / an** with one singular countable noun:
- a ruler, a college, an instrument;
- an apple, an email, an hour.

Use **some** in many affirmative plural sentences: *There are some books on the desk.*

### 3. Uncountable nouns

Uncountable nouns usually have no plural form when used in their general meaning:
- water, milk, bread, rice;
- information, advice, homework;
- equipment, furniture, money.

Do not say *an information* or *many equipment*. Say:
- some information;
- a piece of information;
- a piece of advice;
- a piece of equipment / two pieces of equipment.

To express a quantity, use a container or unit:
- a bottle of water;
- a cup of tea;
- a packet of rice;
- a piece of paper.

### 4. Some, any, much and many

| Word | Typical use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| some | affirmative sentences; offers and requests | We need some paper. |
| any | many questions and negatives | Are there any notebooks? |
| much | uncountable nouns | How much water do we need? |
| many | plural countable nouns | How many students are there? |
| a lot of | countable and uncountable nouns | We have a lot of work. |

Examples:
- There are **some** pens in the box.
- We don't have **any** clean paper.
- How **many** computers are in the room?
- How **much** time do we have?
- There is **a lot of** information in this manual.

In everyday affirmative sentences, *a lot of* is often more natural than *much*: *We have a lot of homework.*

### 5. Reading: Preparing a Classroom

*Before the English lesson, the group monitor checks the classroom. There are twenty students, so the teacher needs twenty worksheets. There is some paper near the printer, but there isn't any coloured paper. The group has a lot of notebooks and a few dictionaries. The teacher asks, “How many dictionaries do we have?” The monitor answers, “We have four.” Then the teacher asks, “How much time is left before the lesson?” There are ten minutes.*

**Gist:** What is the monitor checking?

**Detail:**
1. How many students are there?
2. How many worksheets does the teacher need?
3. Is there any coloured paper?
4. How many dictionaries are there?
5. How much time is left?

### 6. Listening — “At the College Shop”

**Before you listen:** *notebook* — тетрадь; *pack* — упаковка; *enough* — достаточно; *receipt* — чек.

**Listening script for teacher recording (about 45–55 seconds):**

> Customer: Good morning. Have you got any notebooks?  
> Shop assistant: Yes, we have. How many do you need?  
> Customer: Three, please. And I need some paper.  
> Shop assistant: We have white paper in packs of one hundred sheets.  
> Customer: Great. How much is one pack?  
> Shop assistant: It is two euros. Would you like a pen as well?  
> Customer: No, thank you. I have two pens already.  
> Shop assistant: Anything else?  
> Customer: That's all, thank you. Could I have the receipt, please?

**First listening:** What does the customer buy?

**Second listening:**
1. How many notebooks does the customer need?
2. How many sheets are in one pack?
3. How much is the pack?
4. Does the customer buy a pen?
5. What does the customer ask for at the end?

**Language focus:** Find one question with *how many* and one with *how much*. Explain why each is used.

**After listening:** Role-play a similar conversation. Change the items and quantities.

*Teacher note: original script for recording; no MP3 is included in this draft.*

### 7. Practice

**A. Write C (countable) or U (uncountable).**
1. equipment 2. chair 3. information 4. notebook 5. water 6. advice 7. tool 8. homework.

**B. Choose the correct word.**
1. How (much / many) students are in your group?
2. We need (some / any) paper.
3. There isn't (some / any) milk.
4. How (much / many) money do you have?
5. There are (much / many) books on the shelf.

**C. Correct the sentences.**
1. I need an information.
2. How much notebooks do you have?
3. We haven't got some water.
4. There are three equipment in the room.

**D. Complete the dialogue.**
A: Have you got ___ pencils?  
B: Yes. How ___ do you need?  
A: Four, please. And how ___ is this notebook?  
B: It is three euros.

### 8. Self-check

1. Is *furniture* countable or uncountable?
2. Complete: *How ___ computers are there?*
3. Complete: *How ___ time do we have?*
4. Choose: *There isn't (some / any) sugar.*
5. Correct: *two informations.*

**Answer key for teacher**
- Reading: 1 twenty; 2 twenty; 3 no; 4 four; 5 ten minutes.
- Listening: 1 three; 2 one hundred; 3 two euros; 4 no; 5 the receipt.
- Practice A: 1 U; 2 C; 3 U; 4 C; 5 U; 6 U; 7 C; 8 U.
- Practice B: 1 many; 2 some; 3 any; 4 much; 5 many.
- Practice C: 1 I need some information / a piece of information. 2 How many notebooks do you have? 3 We haven't got any water. 4 There are three pieces of equipment in the room.
- Practice D: some; many; much.
- Self-check: 1 uncountable; 2 many; 3 much; 4 any; 5 two pieces of information.

### 9. Independent work

Create a list of 12 things for a study day or a practical class. Mark each noun C or U. Write six sentences using *some, any, much, many* and *a lot of*. Add two questions you could ask a shop assistant.

## Lesson 12. Short Messages, Notes and Informal Emails

**Level:** A1–A2  
**Suggested lesson time:** 80 minutes  
**Focus:** writing short, clear messages for everyday and college situations.

### Learning outcomes

Students can:
- identify the purpose and recipient of a short message;
- structure an informal email with a greeting, reason, key details and closing;
- write a short note about a change of time or place;
- use basic polite phrases and check a message before sending it.

### 1. Lead-in: why do we write?

People write short messages to:
- say they are late;
- ask about a room or timetable;
- explain an absence;
- confirm a meeting;
- send a file or remind someone about homework.

A useful message answers three questions: **Why are you writing? What does the reader need to know? What should happen next?**

### 2. Message structure

A short informal email usually has:
1. **Subject** — a few words describing the topic.
2. **Greeting** — *Hi Alex,* / *Hello Marina,*
3. **Opening** — *I'm writing about…* / *Just a quick message…*
4. **Main information** — what happened, when and where.
5. **Action or question** — what you need from the reader.
6. **Closing** — *Thanks,* / *See you,* / *Best,*
7. **Name**.

Example:

**Subject: English homework**

Hi Marina,

I'm writing about the English homework. I was absent yesterday. Could you send me the page numbers, please? I'll do the exercises tonight.

Thanks,  
Olga

### 3. Useful language

| Purpose | Useful phrase |
|---|---|
| Give a reason | I'm writing because… |
| Explain a problem | I'm afraid I can't… |
| Ask politely | Could you tell me…? |
| Request a file | Could you send me…? |
| Confirm | That's fine. / I can be there at… |
| Apologise | Sorry I'm late. |
| Thank | Thanks for your help. |
| Close | See you tomorrow. / Best wishes, |

Use **please** and **thank you** to keep requests polite. Avoid unclear messages such as *Send it* when the reader may not know what *it* means.

### 4. Reading: A Change of Room

**Message 1**

*Hi everyone,  
Today's English lesson is in Room 214, not Room 108. The lesson starts at 10:15 as usual. Please bring your notebooks.  
Thanks,  
Class monitor*

**Message 2**

*Hello,  
I am in Room 108 now. Thank you for the message. I'll go to Room 214.  
Best,  
Denis*

Questions:
1. What has changed?
2. Has the lesson time changed?
3. What should students bring?
4. Where is Denis now?
5. What will Denis do?

### 5. Listening — “A Message for the Group”

**Before you listen:** *reschedule* — перенести на другое время; *available* — свободный, доступный; *confirm* — подтвердить.

**Listening script for teacher recording (about 45–55 seconds):**

> Hello, this is Anna from Group One. I'm calling about tomorrow's computer class. The teacher has changed the room. We are meeting in Room 305, not Room 203. The class still starts at nine o'clock. Please bring your notebooks and headphones. If you can't come, send me a message before six this evening. I'll confirm the information in our group chat. Thank you. See you tomorrow.

**First listening:** What is the message about?

**Second listening:**
1. Who is speaking?
2. Which room is the new room?
3. What time does the class start?
4. What should students bring?
5. By what time should absent students send a message?

**Language focus:** Identify the phrases that announce a change, give instructions and ask for a response.

**After listening:** Write a two-sentence message to a classmate explaining that a lesson has moved to another room.

*Teacher note: original script for recording; audio production and a player are separate implementation tasks.*

### 6. Writing workshop

**Task A — complete the note.**

You are going to be ten minutes late. Write a message to your classmate. Include:
- an apology;
- the reason;
- your expected arrival time;
- a request to tell the teacher.

**Task B — write an informal email (50–60 words).**

You missed a lesson and need the homework. Write to a classmate. Include:
- a greeting;
- the reason you are writing;
- a polite request for the homework details;
- when you will complete the work;
- a suitable closing.

**Task C — edit the message.**

Original:
*hi. i not come today. send homework. bye*

Improve it by adding correct grammar, punctuation, a polite request and a clear reason or useful detail. Do not add facts you do not know.

### 7. A simple writing checklist

Before sending, check:
- Is the recipient clear?
- Does the subject match the message?
- Have I included the important date, time or room?
- Are the verbs correct?
- Have I used capital letters and full stops?
- Is the request polite?
- Is there a closing and my name?

### 8. Self-check

1. Name two parts of an informal email.
2. Which is more polite: *Send me the file* or *Could you send me the file, please?*
3. What should a subject line tell the reader?
4. Correct: *I am write about the timetable.*
5. Write a 25–35-word message telling a classmate that tomorrow's meeting starts at 11:00 instead of 10:00.

**Answer key / assessment guidance**
- Reading: 1 the room; 2 no, it remains 10:15; 3 notebooks; 4 Room 108; 5 go to Room 214.
- Listening: 1 Anna from Group One; 2 Room 305; 3 9:00; 4 notebooks and headphones; 5 before 6 p.m.
- Self-check: 1 any two of subject, greeting, opening, main information, request/action, closing, name; 2 the second; 3 the topic; 4 I am writing about the timetable. Item 5: accept a clear message with correct time and polite wording.
- Writing rubric (10 points): task completion 0–3; clarity and organisation 0–2; grammar 0–2; vocabulary and polite expressions 0–2; spelling and punctuation 0–1. Minor errors should not erase credit when meaning remains clear.

### 9. Independent work

Write an informal email of 60–70 words to a classmate. Explain that you cannot attend a planned study meeting, suggest another time, and ask which materials you should prepare. Use at least two polite phrases. Check it with the checklist.

## Semester 2 review task

Complete a short integrated task:
1. Write five sentences about what you did last weekend (Past Simple).
2. Make two questions about quantities using *how much* and *how many*.
3. Write a 50–60-word email asking a classmate for information about a missed lesson.
4. Listen to one of the teacher-recorded scripts again and summarise its main point in two sentences.

**Review criteria:** correct use of Past Simple; accurate countable/uncountable forms; clear message structure; ability to identify the main point and key details in a short recording.
