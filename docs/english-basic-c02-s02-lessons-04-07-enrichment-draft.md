# Lesson 4. The History of Physics: People, Ideas and Scientific Progress

**Suggested time:** 90 minutes

## Learning outcomes

Students will be able to:
1. identify the main idea and chronological sequence in a historical science text;
2. use vocabulary for scientific discovery, principles, motion and research;
3. use Past Simple and time expressions to describe events;
4. distinguish a historical contribution from a simplified textbook claim;
5. give a short spoken summary of a scientific development.

## 1. Lead-in

Discuss in pairs:
- Why do people study the history of science?
- Can one discovery change several areas of technology?
- What is the difference between a discovery, an invention and a scientific theory?
- Which scientific ideas have influenced modern transport, communication or manufacturing?

Make a quick timeline of three scientific developments you already know. Do not worry about exact dates yet.

## 2. Vocabulary

| English | Russian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| research | исследование | Scientific research takes time. |
| discovery | открытие | The discovery changed scientific thinking. |
| invention | изобретение | An invention may solve a practical problem. |
| principle | принцип | The principle is used in many explanations. |
| law of motion | закон движения | Newton's laws describe motion in classical mechanics. |
| inertia | инерция | Inertia is a property of matter. |
| acceleration | ускорение | Acceleration describes a change in velocity. |
| mass | масса | Mass is measured in kilograms. |
| force | сила | A force can change an object's motion. |
| theory | теория | A theory explains a range of observations. |
| evidence | доказательство, свидетельство | New evidence can change an explanation. |
| experiment | эксперимент | The experiment tested the hypothesis. |
| contribution | вклад | Her contribution was important. |
| development | развитие | The development of physics influenced engineering. |
| to establish | устанавливать, обосновывать | The experiment helped establish the result. |

### Word building

- discover → discovery → discoverer;
- invent → invention → inventor;
- develop → development → developmental;
- explain → explanation → explanatory;
- measure → measurement → measurable.

### Practice 1. Complete the sentences

Use: *research, discovery, principle, acceleration, evidence, contribution*.

1. The scientist's ______ influenced later work.
2. The team carried out ______ into material properties.
3. The experiment provided new ______.
4. The ______ of a new effect changed the direction of research.
5. The law of inertia is an important ______ in classical mechanics.
6. A change in velocity is related to ______.

### Second reading — detail

### Critical reading note

The source passage is a simplified historical overview and some of its descriptions of relativity are imprecise. For example, special relativity is not accurately described as “primarily a geometrical theory of gravitation”; general relativity is the theory of gravitation. The purpose here is to understand the text's sequence and vocabulary, not to memorise an incorrect definition. Invite students to mark statements that need specialist verification.

## 4. Grammar focus: Past Simple and time expressions

Use Past Simple for completed historical events:
- Galileo **introduced** important ideas.
- Planck **proposed** the quantum hypothesis in 1900.
- Scientists **developed** new methods.

Regular verbs: *develop — developed; study — studied*.  
Irregular verbs: *write — wrote; become — became; find — found; make — made*.

Useful time expressions:
- in the seventeenth century;
- at the beginning of the twentieth century;
- later / afterwards;
- during the following decades;
- by the end of the century.

### Practice 2. Put the verbs into Past Simple

2. Scientists (develop) new experimental methods.
3. The discovery (change) the direction of research.
4. The team (find) evidence for its hypothesis.
5. The new theory (become) influential.

### Practice 3. Put the events in a logical order

a) Later researchers developed and tested new ideas.  
b) A question or problem appeared.  
c) An experiment provided new evidence.  
d) Scientists discussed and refined an explanation.

Explain your sequence using *first, then, after that, finally*.

## 5. Listening: preparing a short science presentation

**Before you listen**

Words: *timeline, contribution, period, explain, source, verify*. Predict: What should a student do before presenting a historical scientific fact?

**First listening — gist**

What are the students preparing?

A) A short presentation about the development of a scientific idea.  
B) A sports report.  
C) A travel booking.

**Second listening — details**

1. What does the teacher ask them to prepare?
   A) a timeline with key dates and ideas  
   B) a list of unrelated words  
   C) a drawing of the classroom
2. What should they do if two sources give different dates?
   A) check a reliable reference and explain the difference  
   B) choose a date at random  
   C) remove every date
3. What should each speaker explain?
   A) why the contribution mattered  
   B) only the scientist's favourite food  
   C) the colour of the laboratory
4. How long should the presentation be?
   A) about two minutes  
   B) twenty minutes  
   C) one hour

**Recording script — reveal after the tasks**

**Student 1:** We have to prepare a short presentation about the history of a scientific idea. I think we should begin with a timeline.

**Student 2:** Good idea. We can show the main people, dates and contributions.

**Student 1:** I found two websites with different dates for one event. Which one should we use?

**Student 2:** Let's check a reliable science reference. We should not choose a date just because it appears first in a search result.

**Teacher:** Exactly. Also, explain why the contribution mattered. A list of names and dates is not enough.

**Student 1:** So we will introduce the problem, describe the key discovery and finish with its influence on later research.

**Teacher:** That's a clear structure. Keep the presentation to about two minutes and include the sources you used.

**Language focus**
- *We should begin with…* — suggestion.
- *I found two websites…* — reporting a research result.
- *We should not choose…* — warning against an unreliable method.
- *its influence on later research* — describing significance.

**After listening**

In pairs, give a two-minute presentation about a scientific development covered in the source passage. Include one date, one contribution, one explanation of its importance and one source-checking statement.

## 6. Writing: a short historical profile

Write 100–120 words about one scientist or one scientific idea mentioned in the source text. Include:
- the period or date;
- the question or problem;
- the contribution;
- its influence;
- one sentence explaining why sources should be checked.

1. Which tense is normally used for completed historical events?
2. What is the difference between a discovery and an invention?
3. Name two time expressions used for historical writing.
4. Why is a timeline useful?
5. Why should a simplified textbook explanation sometimes be checked?

### Teacher key

- Practice 2: 1 studied; 2 developed; 3 changed; 4 found; 5 became.

- Writing rubric (8 points): accurate content 2; chronology 1; significance 1; source awareness 1; organisation 1; grammar 1; vocabulary 1.

**Independent work:** make a five-point timeline from the source passage and write one original Past Simple sentence for each point.

# Lesson 5. The Revolution in Physics: How New Evidence Changes Ideas

**Suggested time:** 90 minutes

## Learning outcomes

Students will be able to:
1. describe a scientific discovery and its consequences;
2. understand vocabulary for radiation, observation, evidence and theory;
3. use Past Simple and Past Continuous to describe events and background actions;
4. identify cause, result and contrast in a scientific narrative;
5. explain why unexpected results should be investigated rather than ignored.

## 1. Lead-in

Discuss:
- Can an experiment produce an unexpected result?
- What should a researcher do if the result does not match the prediction?
- Why can a discovery in one field become useful in another field?
- What is the difference between an observation and an explanation?

## 2. Vocabulary

| English | Russian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| unexpected | неожиданный | The experiment produced an unexpected result. |
| radiation | излучение | Some forms of radiation can be detected by instruments. |
| X-ray | рентгеновское излучение / рентгеновский снимок | X-rays are used in medical imaging. |
| photographic plate | фотопластинка | The plate recorded an image. |
| fluorescent screen | флуоресцентный экран | The screen began to glow. |
| to observe | наблюдать | The researcher observed a change. |
| to detect | обнаруживать | The instrument detected a signal. |
| to investigate | исследовать | The team investigated the result. |
| to reveal | выявлять, обнаруживать | The image revealed the structure. |
| consequence | последствие | The discovery had important consequences. |
| application | применение | Medical imaging is one application. |
| hypothesis | гипотеза | The hypothesis must be tested. |
| reliable | надёжный | The result should be reliable. |
| to repeat | повторять | Other researchers repeated the experiment. |

### Practice 1. Choose the correct word

1. The researcher (observed / observation) an unexpected effect.
2. The instrument helped (detect / detection) the signal.
3. A scientific (hypothesis / hypothetical) must be tested.
4. The discovery had several important (consequence / consequences).
5. Other teams tried to (repeat / repetition) the experiment.

## 3. Reading: the discovery of X-rays

Read the existing source passage “The Revolution in Physics” (Basic 6.2). It describes Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen's discovery of X-rays and later developments in physics.

### First reading — gist

Choose the best summary:
A) An unexpected observation led to further experiments and opened new possibilities for science and medicine.  
B) A scientist designed a new type of aircraft engine.  
C) The text describes how to operate a machine tool.

### Second reading — details

1. What equipment was Röntgen studying?
2. What unexpected effect did he notice?
3. What happened to the fluorescent screen?
4. What happened to photographic plates?
5. Why did other physicists repeat the experiment?
6. Which later scientific developments are listed in the source text?
7. What does the author mean by calling the discovery important beyond its immediate medical value?

### Reading skill: cause and result

Complete using details from the source passage.

| Cause / event | Result |
|---|---|
| Röntgen was studying a discharge tube. | |
| An unexpected effect was observed. | |
| Photographic plates were affected. | |
| Other physicists repeated the experiment. | |

### Critical thinking

The source is a historical narrative. Distinguish:
- the observed effect;
- the name given to the phenomenon;
- the later explanation and applications.

A discovery may be observed before its full explanation is understood. Do not confuse the first observation with all later scientific knowledge.

## 4. Grammar: Past Simple and Past Continuous

Use Past Continuous for an action in progress at a particular past moment:
- Röntgen **was studying** a discharge tube.
- The screen **was glowing** while the experiment was running.

Use Past Simple for a completed event:
- He **noticed** an unexpected effect.
- Other researchers **repeated** the experiment.

Often the two tenses work together:
- While the scientist **was working**, he **noticed** something unusual.
- The screen **was glowing** when he **looked** at it.

### Practice 2. Choose the correct form

1. The scientist (studied / was studying) the tube when he noticed the effect.
2. Other researchers (repeated / were repeating) the experiment after the discovery became known.
3. The screen (glowed / was glowing) while the equipment was operating.
4. The team (recorded / was recording) the result and then discussed it.

### Practice 3. Join the sentences

Use *while* or *when*:
1. The students were reading the report. The teacher entered the room.
2. The instrument was operating. The signal changed.
3. The researcher checked the plate. He noticed an image.

## 5. Listening: an unexpected result in a laboratory demonstration

**Before you listen**

Words: *prediction, result, repeat, record, compare, explanation*. Predict: What steps should a student take when an experiment gives an unexpected result?

**First listening — gist**

What happened during the demonstration?

A) The result was different from the students' prediction.  
B) The class was cancelled.  
C) The students lost their timetable.

**Second listening — details**

1. What did the students expect?
   A) no visible change  
   B) a loud sound  
   C) a change in room temperature
2. What did they do first?
   A) recorded the observation  
   B) changed every setting  
   C) deleted the notes
3. What did the instructor ask them to do?
   A) repeat the procedure under the same conditions  
   B) invent a result  
   C) stop studying the topic
4. What should they avoid?
   A) claiming an explanation before checking the evidence  
   B) writing down observations  
   C) comparing results

**Recording script — reveal after the tasks**

**Student:** We expected the indicator to remain unchanged, but it changed colour during the demonstration.

**Instructor:** Did you record the starting conditions?

**Student:** Yes. We wrote down the values and the time. We did not change the equipment.

**Instructor:** Good. First, repeat the procedure under the same conditions. Then compare the results.

**Student:** Should we write that we know the cause?

**Instructor:** Not yet. Record what you observed, then suggest possible explanations. An observation and an explanation are not the same thing.

**Student:** So we should say that the result was unexpected and that we need another test.

**Instructor:** Exactly. Clear records help other people understand what happened.

**Language focus**
- *We expected…, but…* — contrast between prediction and result.
- *We wrote down…* — reporting a completed action.
- *Not yet* — delaying a conclusion until evidence is available.
- *An observation and an explanation are not the same thing* — scientific caution.

**After listening**

Role-play: one student reports an unexpected result; the other asks about conditions, records and repeatability. Finish with a neutral written observation, not an unsupported explanation.

## 6. Writing: observation report

Write 90–110 words about a fictional classroom demonstration. Include:
- the prediction;
- what was observed;
- what was recorded;
- what should be checked next;
- one Past Continuous and two Past Simple forms.

Do not describe hazardous experiments or give instructions for handling chemicals or radiation.

## 7. Self-check

1. Which tense describes an action in progress in the past?
2. What is an observation?
3. Why should an unexpected result be recorded?
4. What is the difference between a hypothesis and a confirmed result?
5. Why might other researchers repeat an experiment?

### Teacher key

- Practice 1: 1 observed; 2 detect; 3 hypothesis; 4 consequences; 5 repeat.
- Gist: A.
- Reading answers: 1 a cathode-ray discharge tube; 2 an unexpected effect outside the tube; 3 it fluoresced/glowed; 4 they were affected/fogged; 5 to test and demonstrate the finding; 6 accept listed items from the source; 7 because it influenced wider physics and later applications.
- Practice 2: 1 was studying; 2 repeated; 3 was glowing; 4 recorded.
- Practice 3: 1 The students were reading the report when the teacher entered. 2 While the instrument was operating, the signal changed. 3 The researcher noticed an image when he was checking the plate.
- Listening: gist A; details 1 A; 2 A; 3 A; 4 A.
- Self-check: 1 Past Continuous; 2 what was noticed/measured without adding an explanation; 3 so it can be checked and compared; 4 a hypothesis is a proposed explanation to test; 5 to check whether the result can be reproduced.
- Writing rubric (8 points): prediction and observation 2; next step 1; tense control 2; clarity 1; vocabulary 1; organisation 1.

**Independent work:** write five sentences about a discovery using *while, when, suddenly, afterwards* and *as a result*.

# Lesson 6. Automation: Systems, Sensors and Human Control

**Suggested time:** 90 minutes

## Learning outcomes

Students will be able to:
1. explain the basic components of an automated system;
2. understand vocabulary for sensors, feedback, control and programmed actions;
3. describe a process using sequence markers and the Present Simple Passive;
4. identify the main point and specific details in a discussion about automation;
5. discuss benefits and limitations of automation without assuming that a machine can work safely without supervision.

## 1. Lead-in

Discuss:
- Which automated systems do you use every day?
- What is the difference between an automatic device and a robot?
- Why do automated systems need sensors?
- Which tasks should still involve human checking?

## 2. Vocabulary

| English | Russian | Example |
|---|---|---|
| automation | автоматизация | Automation can improve repeatability. |
| automated system | автоматизированная система | The system follows a programmed sequence. |
| program | программа | The program defines the sequence of actions. |
| sensor | датчик | A sensor measures a physical quantity. |
| feedback | обратная связь | Feedback helps the controller respond to changes. |
| controller | контроллер | The controller processes input signals. |
| actuator | исполнительный механизм | An actuator produces movement. |
| control system | система управления | A control system coordinates operations. |
| input | входной сигнал / ввод | The sensor provides input. |
| output | выходной сигнал / результат | The system produces an output. |
| set point | заданное значение | The controller compares the reading with the set point. |
| monitor | контролировать, отслеживать | The operator monitors the process. |
| fault | неисправность | The system reports a fault. |
| maintenance | техническое обслуживание | Regular maintenance is necessary. |
| operator | оператор | The operator checks the status. |

### The basic control loop

**Input / set point → controller → actuator / process → sensor feedback → controller**

A simple example: a temperature-control system compares the measured temperature with a target value and adjusts heating or cooling. Real systems vary in design and complexity.

### Practice 1. Match the component to its function

1. sensor  
2. controller  
3. actuator  
4. program  
5. operator

a) carries out a physical action  
b) monitors the system and responds according to procedures  
c) detects or measures a condition  
d) defines the sequence or logic  
e) processes information and sends control commands

## 3. Reading: automation and production

Read the existing source passage “Automation” (Basic 6.3). While reading, identify:
- the elements of an automated system;
- the role of sensing or feedback;
- examples of automatic equipment;
- the relationship between robots and other production equipment;
- the role of people in automated production.

### First reading — gist

Choose the best summary:
A) Automation combines several techniques and components to control a process or production system.  
B) Automation means using computers only for entertainment.  
C) A robot can always work independently without maintenance or supervision.

### Second reading — detail

1. Which elements of an automated system are named in the source?
2. What does the program element determine?
3. Why is sensing or feedback important?
4. What examples of production equipment are mentioned?
5. Why does the source say that a robot alone may be of limited use?
6. What is a flexible production system?

### Critical thinking

The source text reflects the period in which it was written. Its broad claims about “one hundred per cent automated production” should be discussed critically. Modern automation still requires system design, maintenance, monitoring, cybersecurity, quality assurance and human responsibility. Automation can reduce some risks and repetitive work, but it can also introduce new failure modes.

## 4. Grammar: Present Simple Passive and process sequence

Use the passive when the action or process is more important than the person doing it.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| The sensor measures the temperature. | The temperature is measured by the sensor. |
| The controller processes the signal. | The signal is processed by the controller. |
| The system stores the data. | The data is stored by the system. |

**Form:** am / is / are + past participle.

Sequence markers:
- first;
- next;
- then;
- after that;
- finally.

### Practice 2. Change into the passive

1. The sensor measures the pressure.
2. The controller processes the signal.
3. The system stores the results.
4. The operator checks the display.
5. The software records the warning.

### Practice 3. Describe a simple process

- the measured value is compared with the target;

- the controller sends a command;
- the actuator changes the process.

## 5. Listening: explaining a temperature-control system

**Before you listen**

Words: *target value, reading, compare, controller, heating element, warning*. Predict: What happens if the measured value is different from the target value?

**First listening — gist**

What is the speaker explaining?

A) How a simple automatic temperature-control system works.  
B) How to book a flight.  
C) How to prepare a CV.

**Second listening — details**

1. What does the sensor measure?
   A) the current temperature  
   B) the operator's age  
   C) the room number
2. What does the controller compare?
   A) the measured value and the target value  
   B) two student timetables  
   C) the colour of two cables
3. What may happen if the value is outside the set range?
   A) the controller sends a command or warning  
   B) the system automatically becomes safe in every situation  
   C) the sensor disappears
4. What is the operator expected to do?
   A) monitor the system and follow the approved procedure  
   B) ignore all warnings  
   C) change settings without permission

**Recording script — reveal after the tasks**

**Instructor:** Let's describe a simple temperature-control system. First, a sensor measures the current temperature.

**Student:** Is the measured value sent to the controller?

**Instructor:** Yes. The controller receives the reading and compares it with the target value.

**Student:** What happens if the temperature is too low?

**Instructor:** Depending on the system design, the controller may send a command to the heating element. The system continues to monitor the temperature.

**Student:** And if a warning appears?

**Instructor:** The operator must follow the approved procedure. An automatic system does not remove the need for supervision or maintenance.

**Student:** So the sensor measures, the controller makes a control decision, and the actuator changes the process.

**Instructor:** Correct. That is a simplified control loop.

**Language focus**
- *is measured / is sent / is compared* — Present Simple Passive.
- *depending on the system design* — qualification.
- *must follow* — obligation.
- *does not remove the need for…* — describing a limitation.

**After listening**

Draw a simple block diagram and explain it in five sentences. Use at least three passive forms and two sequence markers.

## 6. Speaking: benefits and limitations

Discuss in groups:
- Which repetitive tasks can be automated?
- How can automation improve consistency?
- What new risks can appear?
- Why are maintenance and operator training important?

Use:
- One advantage is…
- A possible limitation is…
- The system should be checked because…
- Human supervision remains important when…

## 7. Self-check

1. What does a sensor do?
2. What is feedback?
3. Complete: “The signal ______ (process) by the controller.”
4. Name two sequence markers.
5. Why does automation not eliminate human responsibility?

### Teacher key

- Practice 1: 1 c; 2 e; 3 a; 4 d; 5 b.
- Gist: A.
- Reading answers: 1 program, action, sensing/feedback, decision and control elements; 2 what the system does and the step-by-step sequence; 3 it provides information about the process; 4 accept examples from the source; 5 it must be integrated with other equipment and systems; 6 a coordinated group of machines/equipment that can produce different goods with flexible changeover.
- Practice 2: 1 The pressure is measured by the sensor. 2 The signal is processed by the controller. 3 The results are stored by the system. 4 The display is checked by the operator. 5 The warning is recorded by the software.
- Practice 3: sensor detects → controller compares → controller sends command → actuator changes process. Accept technically coherent passive sentences.
- Listening: gist A; details 1 A; 2 A; 3 A; 4 A.
- Self-check: 1 detects/measures a condition; 2 information returned from the process to the controller; 3 is processed; 4 first, next, then, finally; 5 systems require monitoring, maintenance, judgement and response to faults.
- Diagram/explanation rubric (8 points): correct sequence 3; vocabulary 2; passive forms 2; clarity 1.

**Independent work:** write a 100-word description of one automated system you know. Identify its input, controller, output and one limitation. Do not invent technical specifications.

# Lesson 7. Environmental Pollution: Systems, Cycles and Human Impact

**Suggested time:** 90 minutes

## Learning outcomes

Students will be able to:
1. understand vocabulary for the biosphere, natural cycles, pollution and human impact;
2. identify the main claim and supporting details in a scientific reading;

## 4. Grammar: Present Perfect for change and impact

Use Present Perfect to describe a change that happened in the past and remains relevant now:
- Human activity **has changed** many environments.
- Researchers **have developed** new monitoring methods.
- The college **has introduced** a waste-separation system.

**Form:** have / has + past participle.

Compare:
- Past Simple: The college introduced the system last year. (specific completed time)
- Present Perfect: The college has introduced a new system. (present result; time not specified)

## 5. Listening: discussing a workshop environmental review

**Before you listen**

Words: *review, consumption, leak, maintenance, record, improvement*. Predict: Which environmental improvements can a workshop make without changing its main equipment?

**First listening — gist**

What are the speakers discussing?

A) A review of resource use and practical improvements in a workshop.  
B) A new sports club.  
C) A holiday timetable.

**Second listening — details**

1. What did the students review?
   A) electricity and water use  
   B) exam seating  
   C) the college uniform
2. What did they notice?
   A) lights were left on in an empty room  
   B) the workshop was underwater  
   C) all equipment was new
3. What improvement do they suggest?
   A) a checklist for switching off equipment when appropriate  
   B) removing all safety signs  
   C) changing every machine setting
4. What should be done before making a technical change?
   A) consult the responsible staff and approved procedures  
   B) act without permission  
   C) ignore the equipment manual

**Recording script — reveal after the tasks**

**Student 1:** We have reviewed how our workshop uses electricity and water.

**Student 2:** Did you find anything we could improve?

**Student 1:** Yes. We noticed that some lights were left on when the room was empty. We also found that the equipment checklist does not remind users to report leaks.

**Instructor:** Those are useful observations. You can suggest a reminder for lights and add a note about reporting leaks. Do not change equipment or maintenance procedures yourselves.

**Student 2:** We could prepare a short checklist and ask the workshop manager to review it.

**Instructor:** Exactly. Record the current situation first, then compare it with the results after the changes are approved.

**Student 1:** So we should measure or observe the situation before and after, rather than simply saying that the change worked.

**Instructor:** Correct. Evidence makes an improvement report more useful.

**Language focus**
- *have reviewed* — Present Perfect for a completed review with current relevance.
- *were left on* — passive description of a past situation.
- *should prepare* — recommendation.
- *before and after* — comparison over time.

**After listening**

In pairs, prepare a short improvement note with: observed issue, evidence to collect, suggested action, person responsible for approval and a way to check the result.
